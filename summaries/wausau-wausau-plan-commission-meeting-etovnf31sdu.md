## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Plan Commission held its July 21, 2026 meeting covering a public hearing on a conditional use permit for a new assisted living facility at 151 Mackeno Street, approval of a specific implementation plan for 1010 South 16th Avenue (passing 5-1), and a working review of Chapter 1 of the city's comprehensive plan update. The commission also directed that the AI data center topic be brought back for further review at the August meeting before returning to council in September.

## Key Discussions
### Minutes – June 16 and June 23, 2026
A motion to adopt the minutes was made by True Blood and seconded by Barnaman. No discussion occurred and the motion passed unanimously on a voice vote.

### Public Hearing: Conditional Use Permit – 151 Mackeno Street (Assisted Living Facility)
The applicant's representative presented plans for a 25-bed assisted living facility to be built adjacent to the Grant Place 55-and-over apartment building (70+ units), describing the two as partner projects. Underground and surface parking are planned, and utilities are already stubbed in. Staff clarified that the use is classified as 'institutional residential' requiring a conditional use permit in the Downtown Periphery Mixed Use (DPMU) zoning district, and that no action would be taken tonight — a staff report and final approval vote will come next month. No public comment was offered during the hearing.

### Specific Implementation Plan – 1010 South 16th Avenue
Staff presenter Edmonson explained this was the next step following last month's zoning map amendment and general development plan approval for a planned unit development. The SIP covers a building addition and parking lot expansion, with requested exceptions for pavement setback, parking, and landscaping. Staff recommended approval contingent on parcel combination and submission of a revised landscape plan requiring landscaped islands at parking area ends. The commission voted 5-1 to approve.

### Comprehensive Plan Update – Chapter 1 Issues and Opportunities
Sam Wessel from regional planning presented Chapter 1, noting that 56 survey responses had been received so far and the survey closes September 30. The chapter covers demographic background including population trends, household growth, education, and employment data. Commission members raised questions about peer community comparisons (noting Eau Claire has more land to expand into and benefits from Minnesota migration, while Wausau is more landlocked), the needs of youth residents (one commissioner noting council requested emphasis on younger citizens), data sourcing and margin of error, and graph scaling for accessibility. It was noted that Wisconsin DOA estimates Wausau's 2025 population at 40,571. The natural resources chapter is planned for presentation next month.

### AI Data Center – Return to Commission
Commissioner True Blood requested that the AI data center item be placed on the August agenda for further review before returning to the full council in September. He noted that at the council meeting, members had raised concerns about buffer zones, berming, water retention, and noise, and wanted those issues addressed before the September council meeting. Staff sought clarification on how alderperson input would be delivered, and the commission directed that alders communicate their recommendations directly to the planning department rather than attending the commission meeting.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered outside the public hearing. During the public hearing on 151 Mackeno Street, only the applicant's representative spoke. No members of the public offered comment.

## Action Items
- Specific Implementation Plan for 1010 South 16th Avenue approved 5-1, contingent on parcel combination and revised landscape plan submission
- Conditional Use Permit for 151 Mackeno Street (assisted living facility) to return next month with staff report for final action
- Natural resources chapter (Chapter 2) of comprehensive plan to be presented at August meeting
- AI data center item to be placed on August 18 Plan Commission agenda; alderpersons directed to submit recommendations to planning department ahead of that meeting
- Comprehensive plan survey remains open through September 30; staff continuing neighborhood meeting attendance and focus group formation
