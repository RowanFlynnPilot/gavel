## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Capital Improvement Projects Committee held its final 2026 CIP meeting on July 17, 2026, to review ranked project scores and finalize a funding recommendation to forward to the Finance Committee. The committee approved a capital spending recommendation of $587,300, swapping out the Microsoft Server 2025 licensing upgrade in favor of a standing seam metal roof replacement at Public Works Building 2, and removing a previously-funded airport master plan from the list. The outcome reflects ongoing fiscal pressure as the roughly $600,000 annual capital budget has remained flat for years while costs and deferred maintenance continue to rise.

## Key Discussions
### Budget constraints and project ranking overview
Finance staff (Maryanne) explained that the committee's historical funding level is approximately $500,000–$600,000, with a red line drawn on the ranked spreadsheet showing which projects could be funded. She noted the airport master plan was already funded in the 2026 budget and should be removed as a duplicate. She also flagged several critical-ranked projects below the red line, including a leaking roof at Public Works Building 2, Oak Island tennis courts with severe cracking, fire station one apron restoration, and a cybersecurity data tagging project. Maryanne expressed uncertainty about whether the $700,000 architectural design for Fire Station 1 was timely given ongoing police-fire facility discussions from Monday's committee of the whole meeting.

### Airport projects: Runway 13-31 rehabilitation and taxiway extension
Committee members including Timothy How and [inaudible] Alder questioned the funding split on the Runway 13-31 project, noting the total project is approximately $4.9 million over three years with the city's share coming to roughly $374,300, suggesting a closer to 92% federal/state grant coverage rather than the stated 95-5 split. Maryanne noted the discrepancy may be due to engineering costs on the front end being city-funded, and recommended following up with airport manager John Shal and Becker Hoppy. The taxiway D extension was supported as a revenue generator because new hangars would generate land lease revenue and property tax, with the city's share approximately $131,000 on a $1.6 million project. Committee members agreed airport projects with 95-5 FAA splits should not be passed up.

### Jefferson Parking Ramp HVAC and chiller replacement
Chair Barnes raised questions about the city's legal obligations to the condominium association at the Jefferson parking ramp regarding the $225,000 HVAC replacement, noting the ramp's mixed public-private ownership structure. Maryanne suggested this could potentially be an eligible cost under TID 3 or TID 12 project plans and recommended consulting bond counsel. The item remained below the funding line with no funding allocated.

### CCITC Director Klein: Microsoft Server 2025 licensing and cybersecurity
Director Jerry Klein clarified that the $60,000 Microsoft Server 2025 enterprise licensing request is an upgrade — not a renewal — intended to put the latest server OS on machines being replaced in 2027, rather than the older Server 2022. He noted Server 2022 receives critical security updates through October 2031 but no new features after October 2026. On the data loss prevention/tagging request (previously denied last year), Klein explained the current Microsoft DLP tool is limited and problematic, and that a more sophisticated tagging system is needed to prevent PII from being uploaded to AI engines and is required for cyber insurance questionnaire compliance. He also described the security camera archive servers as reaching end of support in fall 2027, serving 530 cameras across city, county, and North Central Healthcare. Committee member Alder How noted the security update window through 2031 as a reason to defer the server licensing upgrade.

### Parks: Oak Island tennis courts, Silven Hill parking lot, playground equipment
Parks Director Jamie Paulie noted Oak Island's six tennis courts are critically cracked and unsafe, but questioned whether all six need replacement given the proliferation of new courts at Newman, Wausau East, Wausau West, and DC Everest. She suggested options including reducing to four or two courts, or converting to 12 pickleball courts to meet high community demand, and recommended the item go back to the Parks Committee for direction. On Silven Hill parking lot — a recurring request for at least eight years — she noted the deteriorating asphalt is required by city code to be paved and that increased summer use from bike trails and rentals makes it more pressing. On playground equipment, she said she would prioritize Westview Terrace over River Highlands due to a cracked slide requiring immediate attention, and offered to reduce the budget request from $125,000 to $75,000 per playground to facilitate grant applications to the Community Foundation. Playground items were not funded in the final recommendation due to low ranking scores.

### Final deliberation and project swap: server licensing out, metal roof in
Timothy How formally proposed removing the Microsoft Server 2025 licensing ($60,000) from the funded list and replacing it with the standing seam metal roof replacement for Public Works Building 2 ($81,000), noting the server upgrade is a medium-priority item with a security update window through 2031 while the roof is actively leaking and causing structural degradation. The committee reached consensus on this swap. Maryanne confirmed the airport master plan ($15,000) was also removed as already funded in 2026, bringing the total recommended capital spending to $587,300. The motion was made by Alder True Blood and seconded by Timothy How.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. Directors and staff present spoke during the meeting at the committee's invitation, but no members of the general public provided comment.

## Action Items

- Staff to follow up with airport manager John Shal and Becker Hoppy to clarify the actual FAA/state funding split on the Runway 13-31 rehabilitation project
- Staff or bond counsel to evaluate whether Jefferson Parking Ramp HVAC replacement is eligible under TID 3 or TID 12 project plans as an alternate funding source
- Parks Director Paulie to bring Oak Island tennis court options (number of courts, pickleball conversion) to the Parks Committee for direction before next CIP cycle
- Parks Director Paulie to explore Public Works department capacity to handle Oak Island paving in-house to reduce cost
- Finance Committee encouraged by committee members to examine whether the approximately $600,000 annual CIP allocation can be increased in future budgets given flat funding amid rising construction costs
- Parks Director Paulie to apply for Community Foundation impact grants for playground equipment if budget is set at $75,000 per playground in a future request
- Airport sign project proponents (Learn Build Fly, flying service) advised to formalize and document financial commitments in writing before resubmitting
