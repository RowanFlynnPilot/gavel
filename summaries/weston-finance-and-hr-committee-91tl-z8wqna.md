## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Finance and HR Committee held its August meeting covering IT department budget review, July financial reports, TIF district updates, and 2027 budget priorities including potential new staff positions and robotic lawnmowers. The committee approved a contract for a final TIF One audit with Hawkins for $5,210 and voted to move forward with a pay grade reclassification recommendation following a closed session. A $4 million local bank loan for the Mountain Bay Police Headquarters was also announced as forthcoming to the full board.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
One public commenter, identified as Jim from Council 53, raised concerns about the lack of staff time quantification in budget reports, specifically calling out the IT department's in-house application development and the pool budget as examples of incomplete financial data. He argued that decisions should be data-driven, noting that the pool budget failed to account for tourism funding, general fund contributions, concession revenues, and staff hours. He also expressed concern that the 2027 budget wish list lacked cost detail and called for a more analytical approach to financial decision-making across the village.

### Approval of Minutes
A motion was made and seconded to approve the minutes from the previous Finance and HR meeting. The motion carried with no noted opposition.

### July Budget Status Report and TIF Reports
Finance staff (referred to as Jessica) explained a significant revenue difference in the water utility fund between 2025 and 2026, attributing it to additional PASS money received in the prior year. On TIF One, Jessica noted that large disbursements reflected grant payouts to Tommy Docks and Oak River under developers' agreements, accelerated because TIF One is closing. Discussion arose about Wandering Springs West properties still held by the original developer (Gold Key Investments) that were supposed to be conveyed to the village; the committee discussed the ongoing legal effort to obtain quitclaim deeds, with Trustee Brent Olson providing historical context. Items 5, 6, and 7 were acknowledged together and the motion carried.

### IT Department Budget Presentation
Technology Services Director Nate Crow presented a detailed overview of his department, noting it is a department of one covering GIS, network, servers, software, and phones. The 2026 operating budget is $141,745, with water and sewer utilities funding approximately 45-46% of his position. Total all-funds cost is approximately $361,000, with the general fund covering 62%. Crow highlighted cybersecurity risks to public water systems from a coordinated national attack originating in Minnesota, and explained $14,000 in outside services budget as a rapid-response cushion. Public Works staff endorsed the in-house Diggers Hotline mapping app, noting it eliminated the need for additional utility staff. Committee members asked about succession planning if Crow were unavailable; he noted Heartland Business Systems is on call for major incidents.

### 2027 Budget Priorities
Finance Director Jessica and another staff member (Jamie) presented a priorities list for discussion. The committee discussed several items including: SAFER grant staffing positions (described as effectively already decided given public support), a new facilities and grounds maintenance position (committee requested a draft job description and discussion of utility cost-sharing, estimated at possibly 50/50 with utilities), robotic lawnmowers (committee expressed interest in starting with one unit to pilot at the safety building rather than two, noting concerns about cut quality), and the contracted mowing service of approximately $25,000/year that could partially offset a new position. The committee agreed to have staff price out all items on the list for upcoming joint budget meetings starting the week of August 24. The TIF One fund balance was discussed as a potential one-time capital funding source following required distribution to taxing jurisdictions.

### Contract for Final Audit of TIF One
Jessica presented a proposal to engage Hawkins auditors for the required final TIF One audit at a quoted price of $5,210, noting they have previously performed TIF audits for the village and have consistently offered competitive pricing. The motion to approve was made and seconded, and carried unanimously with no discussion opposing the selection.

### Employee Appreciation and Standing Monthly Staff Recognition
The committee discussed options for staff recognition, with public works director Michael cautioning that formal recognition programs have previously caused friction among staff who felt their contributions went unnoticed relative to those recognized. Several committee members suggested a standing monthly agenda item for department heads to voluntarily recognize staff accomplishments. The committee decided to table formal action and directed staff to raise the topic at the next all-staff meeting to gather employee input on what recognition approaches they would find meaningful.

### Ethics Function Discussion
The committee chair introduced the concept of establishing a formal ethics function for the village, noting the board of trustees had referred the topic to this committee. An 11-page ethics ordinance from a neighboring municipality (Wausau) was shared as a reference. Given the presence of new committee members, no substantive action was taken; members were asked to review the materials and return with feedback next month. The chair noted the current village ethics policy routes complaints to department heads and does not address elected officials.

### Closed Session and Return to Open Session - Pay Grade Reclassification
The committee moved into closed session under Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(c) to consider pay grade reclassifications for the utility clerk and fleet positions. Upon returning to open session, a motion was made and seconded to approve the reclassification recommendation, and it carried with no noted opposition.

### Staff Remarks - Mountain Bay Police Headquarters Loan
Jessica announced that following the village board's approval of the Mountain Bay Police Headquarters design and bid, the finance department solicited quotes from local banks for a $4 million loan to accelerate funding, as going to market would take several months. She reported the local bank responses were very competitive and said the board would be asked to approve the loan at the next meeting.

## Public Comment
One speaker identified as Jim from Council 53 offered public comment. He raised concerns about incomplete financial reporting, specifically citing the IT department's in-house app development costs lacking staff time quantification, and the pool budget omitting key figures such as the $40,000 tourism contribution, general fund allocation, and concession revenues. He urged the committee to require data-backed financial analysis before making decisions and expressed concern that the 2027 budget wish list lacked cost detail.
