## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Committee of the Whole met on July 13, 2026 to hear three major presentations: a lead service line replacement program update, a proposal to relocate the police department to the UW Stevens Point Marathon campus and convert the existing police station to a central fire station, and a budget process and priorities discussion. The most significant action taken was a 9-1 vote directing staff to pursue a response to Marathon County's RFP for the UW campus property, a move that could reshape multiple city facility needs simultaneously.

## Key Discussions
### Lead Service Line Replacement Program Update and Proposed Mandatory Ordinance
Sean Aged of Community Infrastructure Partners reported that Wausau has replaced over 2,600 lead service lines to date and received more than $20 million in principal forgiveness grants — the most of any Wisconsin community outside Milwaukee. Director Eric Lynman discussed a proposed mandatory lead service line replacement ordinance, noting it would improve Wausau's DNR project priority list ranking and help access 100% principal forgiveness funding in disadvantaged census tracts. Alder Griner expressed strong personal opposition to mandating work inside residents' homes, comparing it unfavorably to radon regulations, while Mayor Doug Diny noted the city already uses special assessments for sidewalks and curbs as precedent. The ordinance was not voted on; it was noted it will go before the utility commission the following day before returning to council.

### Police Department Relocation to UW Stevens Point Marathon Campus / Central Fire Station Conversion
Chief Barnes presented an opportunity to relocate the Wausau Police Department to North South Hall at the UW Stevens Point Marathon campus, which Marathon County is seeking proposals for via RFP. He described the current police department as at capacity — noting the 82nd officer would have no locker — and suggested the existing police building could be converted for use by the central fire department, potentially avoiding a $28 million new fire station construction cost. Director Randy Gro outlined the proposal process, including a potential closed session on August 12 and a not-to-exceed $50,000 budget modification for architectural feasibility studies (FGM Architects at $25,000 and Five Bugles Design at $15,000) that would only be spent if Wausau is chosen. Alder Larson objected, stating he would not support new police or fire facilities until the city's long-neglected fleet facility is addressed, and Alder Tierney echoed those concerns. However, alders Watson, Slonsky, Lukans, Muskowski, Neil, and True Blood all expressed support for exploring the opportunity given the RFP deadline. A motion to direct staff to pursue the RFP response passed 9-1; the budget modification was deferred to the finance committee and full council.

### Budget Process and Priorities
Alders Watson and Lukans, who initiated the committee of the whole, led a discussion advocating for an earlier and more deliberate budget process, suggesting priority-setting discussions begin in April or May rather than the current fall crunch. Watson emphasized the budget is the city's most important policy document and called for more resident communication throughout the year. Finance Director Maryann (appearing remotely) presented projections showing a roughly $1 million structural shortfall for the 2027 budget before the formal process begins, driven by $1.7 million in wage and benefit increases, $300,000 more in the Harder contract, $1.5 million annual motorpool underfunding, and a projected $300,000 transit subsidy increase, against only about $2.4 million in additional levy capacity. She outlined three revenue diversification options: a vehicle registration fee (averaging $25 in 57 Wisconsin communities), a stormwater utility, and [transcript cut off before third option]. Mayor Diny noted the budget timeline calls for council priority-setting in March, which has never occurred.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. Council President Kilian noted no one in the chamber wished to speak and did not read the public comment statement.

## Action Items
- Staff directed to begin drafting a response to Marathon County's RFP for the UW Stevens Point Marathon campus property
- Budget modification (not-to-exceed $50,000 for architectural/engineering feasibility studies) to be brought through finance committee and full council at the August meeting
- Closed session scheduled for August 12 to discuss details of the RFP proposal strategy
- Lead service line replacement mandatory ordinance to go before utility commission the following day (July 14) before returning to city council
- Fleet facility update expected to come before infrastructure committee in August
- Finance Director Maryann's budget revenue and expense projections to be further discussed; committee encouraged to consider earlier budget priority-setting starting in April or May
