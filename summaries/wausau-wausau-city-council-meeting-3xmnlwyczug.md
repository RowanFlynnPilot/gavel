## Meeting Overview
The Wausau City Council held its July 14, 2026 regular meeting, opening with proclamations for the Daughters of the American Revolution centennial, Air Venture Cup race weekend, and a Young Eagles certificate for 10-year-old Hazel Schaefer. The council took significant action on a data center zoning ordinance (amended to add a 200-foot residential setback and passed unanimously), approved a planning option for a North Second Street development with Eminent Development Corporation (7-4), and debated a term sheet for the Riverlife South development by Wangard Partners — though the transcript ends before a final vote on the latter.

## Key Discussions
### Proclamations and Certificate of Achievement
Mayor Diny presented three proclamations: recognizing the Wausau DAR chapter's 100th anniversary (with a reception announced for Sunday at the Marathon County Historical Society Woodson Center), proclaiming Air Venture Cup Race Weekend July 17-19 at Wausau Downtown Airport (with over 120 aircraft expected from 29 states and international participants), and a certificate of achievement for 10-year-old Hazel Schaefer, selected as the EAA's 2.5 millionth Young Eagle flight recipient. Hazel, who has logged 42 hours in 35 aircraft types and is an EAA Chapter 640 member, will take her commemorative flight at Oshkosh on July 22nd.

### Ordinance 26-0706: Data Center Zoning Regulations
After being pulled from the consent agenda at the request of public commenters, the council debated the ordinance creating a new section of zoning code requiring conditional use permits for data centers, with mandatory disclosure of water use, energy demand, wastewater, noise, and sustainability impacts. Assistant City Planner Carrie Edmonds explained the five findings-of-fact standards guiding CUP review and noted the ordinance was developed collaboratively through the Wisconsin Local Government Climate Coalition's data center work group. Alder Watson moved to amend the ordinance to add a 200-foot minimum setback from residential areas, which passed 8-3 after debate; Alder True Blood suggested revisiting the ordinance in future months for stronger protections, while Alder Hani raised concerns about consent agenda placement and asked staff to review legal precedents from data center lawsuits against municipalities. The main motion as amended passed unanimously 11-0.

### Ordinance 26-0705: Rezoning 1010 South 16th Avenue to PUD for Brass Properties LLC
The council considered rezoning a single-family residential parcel to a Planned Unit Development district for commercial expansion by Brass Properties LLC. Council President Terry Kilian stated she would not support the rezoning because all residents who spoke at the June 16th plan commission public hearing opposed it. Alder Martins spoke in favor, highlighting that the proposed lighting fixtures were downward-facing and dark-sky compliant. The rezoning passed 9-2.

### Resolution 26-0710: Planning Option with Eminent Development Corporation
The council debated a planning option for city-owned parcels on North Second Street and East Bridge Street for a proposed 30-50 unit mixed-use development by Eminent Development Corporation, which came out of the Economic Development Committee on a 3-1 vote. Alder Kilian expressed opposition, noting the developer's RFP response sought land valued at $540,000 for $1, and that she would not support additional market-rate housing. Alder True Blood questioned whether the eight parcels — formerly single-family home sites — could be sold for individual home construction instead. ED Committee Chair Alder Hani supported the planning option, citing city-owned properties sitting vacant for over 2,500 days and the need to demonstrate good faith to developers. Alder Wiscowski asked that future packets include community input data. Economic Development Director Randy Fifick clarified an CDBG funding requirement would mean an affordability component in any final development. The planning option passed 7-4.

### Resolution 26-0713: Firefighter Paramedic Apprenticeship Program
The fire chief presented an 18-month apprenticeship program funded entirely by a $3.2 million federal grant in partnership with Northcentral Technical College, creating limited-time city employee positions with no cost to the general fund. Equipment purchased with grant funds (gear, radios, masks) returns to the fire department at program completion; books return to NTC. The memorandum of understanding was updated as recently as the night before to clarify that the program terminates if grant funds are exhausted. Alder Wiscowski noted the previous HR committee meeting had not approved the item due to unresolved questions about benefits packages, now addressed. The resolution passed unanimously 11-0, though Alder True Blood noted concern about the tight timeline that necessitated the suspension of rules.

### Resolution 26-0708: Riverlife South Term Sheet, Wangard Partners
Dave Ryel of Wangard Partners presented a term sheet for a 143-unit market-rate apartment development on the former Hammerblow site at 920 North First Street and 15 Fulton, noting over $2 million in brownfield remediation costs and a TIF incentive structure returning 80% of new tax increment to the developer for up to 17 years (max $8.35 million). Alder Kilian strongly opposed selling land assessed at $1.4 million for $100,000 with additional $4.84 million in incentives, citing widespread public disapproval. Alder Neil countered that the property generates zero tax revenue and the TIF returns only developer-generated increment, not existing taxpayer dollars. Alder Larson raised concerns about market saturation, noting Foundry on Third is currently at 60% occupancy seven months after opening. The transcript ends before a final vote is recorded.

## Public Comment
Five speakers addressed the council. Susan Lang, president of Emerald Properties (750+ rental units), spoke in support of the Wangard Riverlife South development, calling it a 'wise, low-risk' project that 'checks all the boxes.' Ardan Thornhill of Mackenzie Street supported the data center ordinance in principle but asked the council to clarify evaluation standards, taxpayer cost protections, and requested the item be removed from the consent agenda for full public discussion. Linda Kowski submitted written comments read into the record opposing continued apartment development, citing Marathon County's poor record on low-income housing and asking Mayor Diny not to 'rubber stamp' spending proposals. Tom Kilian, 133 East Thomas Street, said he would prefer an outright ban on AI data centers if legally possible, supported the ordinance as a starting point, and urged a setback of at least 200 feet from residential areas. Christine Martins, 1228 Arthur Street, called it 'ludicrous' that data center regulations were placed on the consent agenda and argued the ordinance does not go far enough to protect residents from infrastructure costs and resource depletion.

## Action Items
- Data center zoning ordinance (26-0706) adopted with 200-foot residential setback amendment; staff and plan commission directed to monitor and revisit regulations within months as technology evolves
- City attorney's office requested to review legal precedents from data center lawsuits against municipalities
- Rezoning of 1010 South 16th Avenue to PUD for Brass Properties LLC approved
- Planning option with Eminent Development Corporation for North Second Street parcels approved; staff to include prior community input data in future packets
- Firefighter Paramedic Apprenticeship Program with NTC approved; chief to submit required documentation promptly given expired deadline
- Bargaining agreement with Amalgamated Transit Union Local 1168 (2026-2028) approved
- Council to review consent agenda practices to avoid placing significant policy items without prior notice
- Residents encouraged to participate in the city's comprehensive plan survey to guide future land use decisions
