## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Board of Trustees and Finance/HR Committee held a joint budget workshop — the first such joint meeting in several years — focused on educating board members about governmental fund accounting, reviewing equalized values and levy limits for the 2027 budget cycle, and beginning preliminary budget discussions. Key decisions included approving a $1.29 million waterworks revenue bond for a water system project and voting to keep trustee compensation unchanged for the 2027–2029 term at $7,200 for trustees and $8,400 for the village president.

## Key Discussions
### Resolution 2026-023: Waterworks Revenue Bonds
The board approved a resolution authorizing the issuance of up to $1,291,283 in waterworks system revenue bonds. Staff explained that the broader project totals approximately $4.3 million but the village received roughly $3 million in principal forgiveness through a state safe drinking water loan, and an additional $272,460 was removed from the project amount due to PFAS class action lawsuit proceeds. The interest rate on the borrowing is 0.365% with total interest of approximately $333,000. The motion was approved unanimously by the board; the Finance/HR committee did not vote separately as staff confirmed this was a board-only item.

### Governmental Fund Accounting and Budget Process Presentation
Finance Director Jessica provided an extensive educational presentation on governmental fund accounting, covering the general fund, special revenue funds, debt service, capital projects, and proprietary/enterprise funds for water, sewer, and storm. She explained the modified accrual basis of accounting, why long-term liabilities and receivables such as the Mountain Bay repayment do not appear on fund balance sheets in the traditional sense, and the distinction between the capital improvement program and the capital projects fund. Village President Maloney noted the timeline requires budget adoption at the November board meeting to meet county tax bill printing deadlines.

### Levy Limits, Equalized Value, and Net New Construction
Staff presented the state's August 11 equalized value report showing the village's net new construction at approximately $31.3 million, representing a 1.624% increase — the maximum allowable levy increase for 2027. The village's total equalized value change was 9%. Staff noted that fixed cost increases such as a workers' compensation modifier increase from 0.86 to 1.17 (a roughly $30,000 hit), Mountain Bay cost increases, and stagnant state aids like cable franchise fees and personal property aid effectively consume most or all of the allowable levy increase before any discretionary spending decisions are made. Staff also flagged uncertainty around how the TIF 2 closure — expected to yield roughly $400,000 in additional levy capacity — will be treated by the state on the levy limit worksheet.

### Expenditure Restraint Program
Staff explained the village currently receives $138,000 under the state expenditure restraint program — $88,000 in the general fund and $50,000 in capital — and expressed concern that the TIF 2 closure could push general fund expenditures above program thresholds and disqualify the village. A potential strategy discussed was moving general transportation aid and associated street maintenance expenditures out of the general fund into Fund 42 to keep general fund expenditures within restraint limits. Staff acknowledged the rules have significant complexity and work-arounds but emphasized compliance.

### 2027 Budget Priorities Discussion
Trustees debated whether the stated budget priorities aligned with community priorities identified in the comprehensive plan, where road maintenance (86% importance, 13% satisfaction) and public safety topped the list. Trustee Clark questioned whether items like a robotic lawnmower reflected village priorities or staff wish-list items, and suggested reframing such items around staff retention and reallocation rather than equipment purchases. Trustee Ermeling and others raised the process concern that discussing wages before knowing available revenues was backwards. A citizen Finance/HR committee member requested data on the number of employees at various pay ranges, supervisor-to-employee compression, merit pay examples, and the total all-in cost per 1% salary change. Staff confirmed SAFER-funded firefighters (approximately 68% of their cost) would migrate to the general fund upon program transition, representing a significant budget pressure.

### Wages, Benefits, and Health Insurance Update
Staff reported that Spectrum (the village's health insurance consortium) has preliminarily estimated a 15% premium increase for 2027, though last year's preliminary estimate of 20% came in at negative 5%. The budget currently carries a 10% placeholder. Staff will conduct a form-fire analysis to price the village independently as a contingency. A 3% COLA placeholder has been included for staff wages along with a merit and market component. The average hourly wage is approximately $63,382 and average salary is approximately $88,724, with the highest salary at $127,000. Trustees discussed alternatives to straight percentage raises including HSA contribution increases, additional holidays (Martin Luther King Day, Juneteenth, Veterans Day), and 4x10 work schedules. The village has an 85/15 premium split with employees and has absorbed premium increases the past two years without passing costs to employees.

### Trustee Compensation for 2027–2029 Term
Village President Maloney stated he did not believe trustee compensation needed to change, noting trustees earn $7,200 per year and the president earns $8,400 — rates set approximately eight years ago after 24 years with no adjustment. He cited comparators including the Town of Weston chair at $14,000, the City of Schofield at $12,000–$13,000, and Marathon County supervisors at $4,600. The Finance/HR committee moved and seconded to leave compensation unchanged for the 2027–2029 term; the motion carried. The full board then made the same motion, which also passed unanimously.

### Capital Improvement Plan Review
Staff reviewed the 2026–2027 capital improvement fund accounts covering facilities, streets, and equipment. Most 2026 projects are already underway with engineering and contracts in place. A $1 million line item for the Howland project was identified as a coding error — correctly budgeted but placed under 'workers comp' rather than 'contracted services/street and curb.' A $100,000 placeholder was included for pool leak detection, described as a rough estimate pending ongoing exploration. Staff confirmed no capital expenditures move forward without final board approval regardless of CIP inclusion.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment: Jim Pensel, 512 [address partially inaudible]. He expressed concern about the 6 p.m. (originally 5:30 p.m.) start time limiting public participation and asked future meetings be held at 6 p.m. He also raised concern that no formal goals agenda item was included and that costs for the staff/director wish list were not in the packet. Most critically, he argued that discussing pay increases as the first budget item was premature and inappropriate given that revenue figures and mandated costs are not yet known, and that citizen-identified priorities like public safety and streets should come first.
