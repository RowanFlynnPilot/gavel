## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance, and Property Committee met to accept the 2025 annual audit and comprehensive financial report, advance 2027 budget priorities, and receive updates on property redevelopment and fleet/rolling stock policy. The committee voted unanimously to accept a clean audit opinion from Baker Tilly, approved budget priority guidelines for 2027 including a 3% fee increase baseline, and deferred action on a River Drive campus proposal to a joint meeting with the Extension, Education, and Economic Development Committee tentatively scheduled for the following Thursday. The meeting is significant to residents because it shows Marathon County in a strong financial position with a $53 million general fund balance and 74% fund balance ratio, while also revealing ongoing challenges in capital infrastructure funding and fleet replacement.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment – River Drive Campus Proposal (New Heights Climbing Gym)
Chair Gibbs announced the committee would take no action on the River Drive proposal at this meeting, deferring it to a joint meeting with the Extension, Education, and Economic Development (EEDC) Committee tentatively scheduled for the following Thursday at 3:00 p.m. Three public speakers — Kim Winterhoff (local business owner and housing provider), Steve Arnold (resident at 739 Jefferson Street), and Dwayne Ditmar (climbing facilitator at the Greenheck Turner Community Center) — each spoke in favor of the proposal for New Heights Climbing Gym on the River Drive property, citing community benefit, multi-generational appeal, and economic development potential. Gibbs confirmed no board members had conflicts with the joint meeting date.

### Approval of Minutes from July 8th Meeting
The committee moved and seconded approval of the July 8th meeting minutes without discussion. The motion carried unanimously.

### Acceptance of 2025 Audit and Annual Comprehensive Financial Report
County Administrator Lance Leonard praised Finance Director Sam and her team and introduced John Rider of Baker Tilly, the county's auditor of over 20 years. Rider reported a clean (unmodified) opinion, only two audit adjustments (both in the airport fund, down from 10 in 2024), and final issuance on June 29 — one month ahead of the prior year. The county's general fund balance rose $2.5 million to $53 million, with an unassigned balance of $37 million and a fund balance ratio of 74% of expenditures (approximately 9 months of operating reserves). Rider noted revenues came in $8.2 million over budget driven primarily by sales taxes and investment income, and the county received a GFOA certificate of achievement for its Annual Comprehensive Financial Report. The only internal control finding was the longstanding note that Baker Tilly prepares the financial statements — a finding present in roughly 90% of similar audits. A prior finding regarding decentralized checking accounts was resolved. Supervisor Watson asked about the swing between assigned and unassigned fund balances, and Sam explained it was driven by the absence of a CIP transfer in 2025 and the updated fund balance policy. The motion to accept the audit report carried unanimously.

### 2027 Budget Development – New Position Requests and Policy Overview
Administrator Leonard outlined new position requests and reorganizations from 10 departments, stating he does not anticipate substantial budget impact beyond budgeted compensation increases. Departments involved include Health, Medical Examiner, Administration, Social Services (including a proposed graduated hire of five social workers and one lead for the Children's Long-Term Support program), Solid Waste, Clerk of Court, Sheriff's Office, Facilities, District Attorney, and Parks. Leonard emphasized the message to departments was to realign existing resources rather than seek new levy-funded positions. Vice Chair Pool raised concerns about large fee increases hitting residents all at once and suggested phasing or flagging the largest increases; Leonard agreed and noted a similar step-increase approach was used previously for solid waste fees.

### Property Redevelopment Update – River Drive Campus and Thomas Street Buildings
Staff reported that the RFP for the Thomas Street building received no responses and suggested the committee consider authorizing listing the property with a real estate agent via three quotes or a brief RFP per county ordinance. Chair Gibbs expressed urgency, stating the county should move as expeditiously as possible to sell Thomas Street and stop paying utility bills there. Staff noted the Marathon County Employees Credit Union, which occupies part of the building, had experienced construction delays at their new location and their move-out timeline was uncertain, though summer or next month was possible. The River Drive proposal discussion was deferred to the joint EEDC meeting. Gibbs also requested that staff clarify what equipment/FF&E would remain at the River Drive property under the proposal before next week's meeting.

### Resolution Establishing 2027 Budget Priorities and Guidelines
Administrator Leonard presented a draft resolution based on results of a board-wide 'bucket challenge' budget prioritization exercise. Top discretionary priorities identified by supervisors were: child care workforce program, PC replacement (~80% funded in the exercise), crisis response expansion, and airport service expansion. Supervisor [inaudible] asked whether the listed categories were mandatory and how additional items could be added; Gibbs and Leonard clarified that standing committees surface priorities from their respective departments. Supervisor Scott (Vice Chair Pool) requested that dollar figures accompany percentage references in the resolution for transparency; Leonard confirmed compensation increases total approximately $1.125 million (range adjustment plus step increases averaging roughly 2-3%) and health insurance costs approximately $775,000 (8% increase split between employer and employee). The motion to approve the resolution and advance it to the full board for consideration that evening carried unanimously.

### Review of Rolling Stock Policy and Funding
Staff member Chris presented the history of the rolling stock/fleet funding policy, noting that costs for vehicles and equipment — particularly marked sheriff's vehicles (Dodge Durangos) and plow trucks — are outpacing the CPI-based funding formula, which will decrease to approximately 4.1% next year. Administrator Leonard acknowledged that zero CIP dollars rolled over in 2026, leaving no funds for squad car purchases. Chair Gibbs cautioned that underfunding capital needs has historically led to costlier deferred maintenance, citing past issues with the jail and library. Supervisor Watson asked whether departments maintain depreciation schedules for vehicles; Chris confirmed fleet management is not centralized in a dedicated staff role. Gibbs suggested evaluating fleet utilization standards (e.g., minimum mileage thresholds) to right-size the fleet. A new supervisor asked whether a fleet manager position was warranted. Leonard agreed the board needs a formal conversation about service level expectations for the sheriff's fleet. No action was taken; Chris was asked to bring more information to the August meeting.

### Year-to-Date Budget Report (April 2026)
Finance Director Sam presented the April 2026 year-to-date budget report. The payroll benchmark is 31.42% of budget, and all departments are operating within their expenditure budgets with no red flags. She noted that tax deed property activity is beginning to show in revenue and loss lines, investment income allocated to the general fund stands at $380,952, and a minor negative expenditure of approximately $3,575 in her own finance budget is a prior-year deferred amount being cleared. Year-to-date interest income across investment accounts totals $3,997,684. The committee had no significant questions.

## Public Comment
Three speakers offered public comment in support of the New Heights Climbing Gym proposal for the River Drive campus: (1) Kim Winterhoff, a local business owner who provides housing to traveling healthcare professionals, argued the gym would fit Wausau's identity as an emerging hub for extreme sports and attract visitors who currently drive hours to Green Bay or Minneapolis; (2) Steve Arnold, of 739 Jefferson Street, highlighted climbing as a multi-generational, inclusive, and problem-solving sport suitable for all ages and abilities; (3) Dwayne Ditmar, climbing facilitator at the Greenheck Turner Community Center, noted existing demand at the current small facility and expressed enthusiasm for a larger, modern gym. All three urged the committee to approve the River Drive proposal.
