## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held an educational meeting on July 23, 2026, focused primarily on a TIF joint review board annual report, proposed zoning amendments related to energy projects and data centers, and a 2027 budget prioritization 'bucket challenge' exercise. No final budget votes were taken, but supervisors physically allocated poker chips representing $2.3 million in projected new revenue across spending priority buckets to inform the upcoming budget guidelines resolution, which will go to the HR Finance and Property Committee and then the full board the following Tuesday.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment — TIF Taxpayer ROI
Edgar resident Peter Weinhank spoke for three minutes urging the board to amend Resolution 36-25 to explicitly define 'taxpayer benefit' as including taxpayer return on investment (ROI). He illustrated his concern using TID Number 4 in Edgar, saying that as a homeowner he would pay a $7,400 subsidy to two companies and would not break even until 2060 at age 104, information he said was never presented to the joint review board. He argued that decision-makers lack the taxpayer ROI data needed to give taxpayers equal priority in TIF decisions.

### 8A: Proposed Zoning Amendments — Energy Projects and Data Centers
Supervisor Ritter deferred to CPZ Director Simmons, who explained that three amendments to Chapter 17 of the zoning code are under discussion by the Environmental Resources Committee: one each for battery energy storage systems, data centers, and large energy generating facilities. A public hearing is scheduled for August 4th at the monthly ERC meeting. Simmons distributed handouts on the proposed drafts and directed supervisors to email Administrator Leonard with questions. Vice Chair Dickinson requested that the handouts also be emailed to supervisors attending virtually, which Simmons agreed to do.

### 8B: TIF Joint Review Board Annual Report — Resolution 36-25
Finance Director Sam reported that she attended 13 joint review board meetings for 11 municipalities between July 2025 and July 2026, and that no new TIDs or amendments arose requiring use of the 10-question criteria in Resolution 36-25. Six TIDs closed during the period, with the City of Wausau closure generating $257,238 returned to the county. Supervisor Robinson asked whether taxpayer ROI is calculated under the new criteria; Sam said it is considered but not necessarily formally calculated, as some benefit is subjective. Supervisor Leur asked about TID closure proceeds and confirmed this was the same report presented to the EED committee on March 5, 2026. Supervisor G asked whether any TIDs were distressed; Sam said some operate in deficit but none are of serious concern given anticipated future growth.

### 9C1A: Resolution R-39-26 — Serenity Broadband Expansion Grant
The board voted on a resolution supporting Serenity's application to the state of Wisconsin broadband expansion grant program. Motion was made by Supervisor Robinson and seconded by Supervisor Conway. The motion carried but was noted as not unanimous by Chair Gibbs.

### 9C2A: Resolution 40-26 — Food Share Quality Assurance Staffing (One Big Beautiful Bill)
Supervisor Leur raised extensive questions about the resolution to amend the 2026 budget to receive state funding (Act 116) for new Food Share quality assurance positions mandated by the federal HR1 legislation. She asked about long-term sustainability if emergency funding lapses, October SNAP reimbursement rate changes, January Medicaid cuts, and the community's growing food insecurity. Administrator Leonard and HHS Director Christa explained that the positions are required to meet a federal mandate to review 25% of cases, that the state is planning to continue funding through 2027 contracts, that the county has no tax levy available to backfill if funding ends, and that the October reimbursement rate change would hit the state—not the county. Supervisor Sundowski asked about fraud and waste nationally; Christa explained the error rate calculation methodology and noted Wisconsin performs well below the national average. Supervisor Leur indicated she would support the resolution despite her concerns.

### 8C: 2027 Budget Prioritization — Bucket Challenge
Administrator Leonard led an interactive exercise in which supervisors allocated poker chips—each representing $50,000, with $2.3 million total in new projected revenue—across labeled spending priority buckets: employee compensation ($1.1M), health insurance ($750K), union contract and elected official wages ($350K, mandatory red chips), crisis response expansion ($200K), airport minimum revenue guarantee ($150K), child care workforce expansion ($50K), and IT/PC replacement ($100K). Supervisors discussed adding buckets for homelessness partnerships, opioid-funded CART expansion, a fieldhouse parks program, a county child care facility, and property tax reduction. Supervisor Pul asked whether not funding new programs and returning money to taxpayers was an option; Leonard confirmed it was. Leonard noted that mandatory expenses alone total approximately $2.2 million of the $2.3 million available. Results will be tallied—including virtual responses via email—and presented to HR Finance and Property Committee the following week before Tuesday's voting meeting.

## Public Comment
One speaker signed up for public comment: Peter Weinhank of Edgar, who spoke for three minutes. He argued that Marathon County's joint review board representatives lack taxpayer ROI data when evaluating TIF proposals, citing TID Number 4 in Edgar as an example where he said a $136,000 homeowner would pay a $7,400 subsidy and not break even until 2060. He urged the board to amend Resolution 36-25 to define 'taxpayer benefit' as explicitly including taxpayer ROI.
