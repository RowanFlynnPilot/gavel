## Meeting Overview
The Kronenwetter Village Board held its August 10, 2026 meeting with six of seven trustees present (Stowell absent/excused). The board approved two significant actions: eliminating the village's oak wilt ordinance and reimbursement policy, and passing a one-year moratorium on new data center development applications to allow planning and utility committees time to study the issue. The meeting also featured extensive financial updates on the village's tax increment districts, equalization values, and upcoming property reassessment process.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment - Written Submissions
Two written public comments were read into the record. Lamont Yager of Civil Road submitted a lengthy letter requesting enforcement of oak wilt ordinance 389-1 against the owner of 1916 Civil Road, asking police enforcement of 389-2 regarding a dead tree defined as a nuisance, and calling for an investigation of Village President Joling for alleged obstruction of justice, accusing him of being 'petty, tyrannical and vindictive.' President Joling noted that the tree in question is being handled following administrator Deval's contact with the property owner, who is waiting until after August 15th to cut the oak. A second letter was entered into the minutes without being read aloud due to length.

### Police Chief Report
The police chief (referred to as 'Terry') reported on several notable incidents: a high number of domestic violence calls in July including one involving ten felony charges and three misdemeanors with a weapon; a 12-year sentence handed down in the Maple Ridge Road aggravated battery case, with the department receiving praise from the district attorney; and a high-speed pursuit of an attempted homicide suspect that came through Kronenwetter at speeds exceeding 120 mph, with KPD participating under their pursuit policy. Trustee Coyle asked about body camera status; the chief confirmed the department has had body cams since approximately 2021-2022, currently using updated units compatible with their in-car systems, with storage through City-County IT rather than higher-cost providers like Axon.

### Village Administrator Report
Administrator Deval welcomed new Community Development Director Nathan Samot, who began the previous Thursday. He updated the board on several matters: the Wausau School District superintendent plans to attend the October 12th board meeting to discuss a possible referendum; a meeting is planned with Green Tree regarding the Mortensen International Golf Course area and access issues; a lawsuit by St. Teresa Church over an open pit sand mine decision is pending a new judge after the original recused herself, with the village's attorney suggesting it may be thrown out; and an Act 10 update was referenced with no immediate action planned. Deval also noted that TID 1's prospective distribution center tenant may begin construction as early as September or October.

### Revaluation Update - Village Assessor Report
The administrator presented on behalf of the assessor, explaining that letters are being mailed today to all property owners informing them of an upcoming exterior revaluation process beginning the week of August 24th. A field team of five individuals from Associated Appraisal will survey the entire village. Property owners need take no action; the revaluation will affect December 2027 tax bills, not 2026. Assessment values are expected to need to increase on average approximately 40% to reach full value, as the current assessment ratio has fallen to 63%. The village must complete this revaluation by end of 2027 or the state will conduct it at two to three times the cost in 2028.

### Finance Director Report
Finance Director John presented preliminary 2027 budget figures from the state. The village's equalized valuation reached nearly $1.2 billion, a $100 million or 9.4% increase, but only $8.8 million is net new construction, meaning the assessment ratio dropped from 69% to 63%. The net new construction limit means the tax levy can technically only increase by about $12,000 for 2027, which does not cover staff raises or basic costs. TID 1 saw a concerning $2.5 million valuation reduction — partly from the local assessor's obsolescence adjustments on two parcels and partly from an unexplained $1.4 million state correction the finance director is still awaiting explanation on — meaning the general fund advance to TID 1 could rise from roughly $125,000 to as much as $200,000 in 2027. On a positive note, the village has $49 million remaining under its general obligation debt limit and still has room under the 12% TID valuation cap, with the finance director flagging a potential future TID 5 to help finance a new water tower.

### Consent Agenda - July 27, 2026 Village Board Meeting Minutes
Trustee Coyle moved and Trustee Mortensen seconded approval of the July 27, 2026 meeting minutes. The motion passed unanimously with no discussion.

### Oak Wilt Ordinance Elimination
The board discussed eliminating the village's oak wilt ordinance (389-1) and its associated reimbursement policy, following a unanimous recommendation from the CLIP committee on August 3rd. Key reasons cited included: the reimbursement fund has been depleted for several years, the village has no certified tree inspector on staff or under contract, and oak wilt is so prevalent throughout the village and surrounding municipalities that enforcement is not feasible. Trustee Solheim raised concern about losing the April-through-August pruning restriction window and its value for developers; staff noted the DNR classifies Marathon County in the north zone with a shorter April 15–July 15 restriction. The board agreed that DNR guidelines would be linked on the village website. Trustee Coyle moved and a trustee seconded to eliminate both the ordinance and reimbursement policy; the motion passed unanimously.

### Resolution 2026-011 - Temporary Moratorium on New Data Center Development
The board considered a one-year moratorium on new data center development applications, referring the matter to the planning commission and utility committee for study of zoning, utility, infrastructure, and land use implications. Administrator Deval noted there is significant misinformation circulating about data centers and the village simply needs better information before acting on any application. Nathan Samot flagged a minor typographical error in the resolution. Trustee Coyle moved and Trustee Mortensen seconded; the resolution passed 7-0 on a roll call vote. It was noted that Marathon County is considering similar action covering townships, and each municipality must pass its own.

### Consideration of Items for Next Agenda
Nathan Samot discussed the appeals process for conditional use permits, stating his research found no villages where CUP appeals go to the village board and recommending the matter be referred to the planning commission, partly because two board members sit on the planning commission, creating impartiality concerns. The board will also receive the 10-year Capital Improvement Plan (CIP) for the first time at the next meeting, after it completes review at the APC. Deval indicated funding mechanisms and affordability analysis will be ready at that time.
