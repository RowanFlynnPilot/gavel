## Meeting Overview
The Community Life & Public Safety Committee of the Village of Weston met on August 3, 2026, addressing code enforcement aging cases, overnight parking ordinance reform, electric scooter/e-moto enforcement challenges, and two ordinance amendments. The committee approved an ordinance establishing procedures for storage and disposal of property seized in nuisance abatements, and voted to forward an animal fancier licensing amendment to the village board, while tabling parking regulation changes pending cost and criteria details.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of Minutes from Last Meeting
A resident (Jim) noted during public comment that the minutes incorrectly showed 'yes' under 'present' for vacant committee seats. The mover modified the motion to approve minutes with that correction removed. The motion passed unanimously.

### Code Enforcement Update
Building Inspector Roman Magcguire and Planning Director Jennifer Higgins reported on aging cases, noting 26 open complaints in July with 13 already closed. Specific discussion covered the Newport Avenue chronic hoarding property (cleaned up for the fourth time in eight years, with six citations issued this cycle), a 2021 Ross Avenue junkyard case complicated by the property owner's death and her dementia, and the West Marketplace at 4020 Scoffield Avenue where dumpster enclosure violations have been open nearly two years with little visible progress. The police chief noted the SRO partnership has been productive. Committee members asked about citation payment rates and whether escalating bond amounts could deter repeat violators; staff agreed to pull court citation data for the next meeting. The committee also discussed notification letter timing issues given mail now routing through Green Bay.

### SAFER Fire Department Update
The SAFER chief reported the board declined to hire six requested personnel, opting instead to explore three positions. The chief emphasized frustration over the ISO rating impact: residents living within five miles of a fire station but more than 1,000 feet from a hydrant are seeing insurance increases of $700–$800, far exceeding the roughly $50 annual tax increase that a failed referendum would have cost. The chief noted the town of Weston considered leaving the district over insurance complaints but appears unlikely to do so after a recent meeting. The lost 'water haul credit' was identified as the key ISO rating driver.

### Mountain Bay Metro Police Department Update
The police chief reported the K9 raffle is scheduled for September 12th at Klux, and Monster Bash is October 9th at DC Everest Middle School, with $12,000 in donations already secured. The chief also noted the new police building came in under budget, and an intergovernmental agreement between Rothschild and Weston is expected within a month. A crisis response training was held at the middle school the prior week.

### Overnight Parking Regulations – Section 82.107
Public Works Director Michael Wolski proposed adding signage on specific problem streets for daytime/alternating parking restrictions to aid snow plowing, in addition to a village-wide overnight ban aligned with neighboring municipalities (2 a.m. to 6 or 7 a.m.). The police chief pushed back on a patchwork approach, arguing uniform rules are easier to enforce and that officers struggle with varying ordinances across municipalities. Committee members raised concerns about criteria for placing streets on the restricted list, signage costs (estimated roughly $100 per street), and equitable treatment of residents. The committee reached consensus to table the issue to the September 8th meeting, directing staff to return with cost estimates and clear criteria for street selection.

### Amendment to Section 50.103 – Nuisance Abatement Storage and Disposal (Ordinance 26-013)
Jennifer Higgins presented the amendment to formalize a 30-day property retention period for items seized during non-summary nuisance abatements, allow immediate disposal of obvious garbage with no apparent value, and enable surplus sale of items of value unclaimed after 30 days. The amendment was prompted by the Newport Avenue cleanup where three dumpsters of material are currently being held at village expense. A committee member raised the question of when seized property legally becomes village possession, particularly given the property owner had attempted to retrieve items during the cleanup. The motion to recommend Ordinance 26-013 passed unanimously and advances to the village board.

### Amendment to Section 10.123 – Animal Fanciers (Ordinance 26-014)
Higgins explained that an animal fancier license application triggered a review revealing the Plan Commission no longer needs to be involved under the current zoning code, and that this committee is better suited to handle licensing. The amended ordinance shifts the hearing process to this committee, with humane officers providing recommendations. Trustee Brent Olson raised concern about the lack of an appeal mechanism short of circuit court; staff and the village attorney noted no other appeal body exists, and Olson was encouraged to raise the issue at the village board level. The Plan Commission will hear one pending application next week before the ordinance takes effect. The motion to recommend Ordinance 26-014 to the village board for its August 17th meeting passed, with one audible 'nay' vote noted.

### Electric Scooter/E-Moto Ordinance Discussion
The committee discussed two resident emails from young athletes who use scooters to get to practice, and broader community feedback including a petition. The police chief expressed strong skepticism, noting e-motos traveling at high speeds, riders fleeing officers (including a pursuit that evening), and groups of masked riders gathering in parking lots. He argued the village may be leading the state on e-moto regulation and cautioned against following other municipalities' models without careful consideration of speed classification and enforcement practicality. A committee member flagged that an attorney-resident suggested the current e-moto definition may inadvertently include fully electric cars like Teslas; staff agreed to research clarifying language. The committee decided not to change the existing ordinance at this time, directed staff to draft language clarifying that licensed motor vehicles are excluded, and discussed possibly publicizing the issue for public input at the September meeting.

## Public Comment
One speaker, Jim (address given as 52nd Street), offered public comment. He raised three issues: (1) a correction to meeting minutes showing 'yes' for vacant seats, suggesting dashes instead; (2) concerns about aging code enforcement cases, specifically the nearly two-year-old complaint at 4020 Scoffield Avenue (West Marketplace) involving dumpster enclosure violations, calling for accountability and transparency; and (3) questions about enforcement of the e-moto/scooter ordinance, including how officers would audit speed or wattage specs, and whether the ordinance could inadvertently restrict street-legal electric motorcycles like the Harley-Davidson LiveWire.
