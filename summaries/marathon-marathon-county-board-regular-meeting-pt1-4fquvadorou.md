## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held its regular meeting on August 25, covering several significant items including a contested infrastructure committee chair appointment, amendments to battery energy storage and large energy facility ordinances, a data center moratorium, and a pivotal vote on whether to proceed with Phase 3 of a new county highway facility estimated to potentially cost up to $60 million. The meeting was significant for residents as it set new zoning rules for battery storage systems, established a temporary moratorium on data centers, and moved the controversial highway garage project forward toward competitive bidding.

## Key Discussions
### Consent Agenda C8 through C13C1 (excluding C11A)
Motion by Supervisor Rosenberg, seconded by Supervisor Jeang. Supervisor Arstead requested C11A be pulled for a separate vote. A supervisor also briefly confused this agenda block with Resolution 49-26 (highway facility), which Chair Gibbs clarified was a separate item. The remaining consent agenda passed unanimously.

### C11A - Infrastructure Committee Chair Appointment
The contested appointment pitted Supervisor Griner (a second-term supervisor) against Supervisor Gao (a first-term supervisor serving as vice chair of infrastructure). Supervisor Arstead argued Gao had proven himself through his response to the Ringle tornado and his experience as a supervisor in Dane County. Vice Chair Dickinson echoed support for Gao, noting Gao had chaired meetings in Dickinson's absence and was more experienced overall. Chair Gibbs and Vice Chair Boots defended Griner, citing a tradition of not placing first-term supervisors as committee chairs. Supervisor Drebeck suggested the matter go to the Rules Review Committee in the future. The motion to approve Griner as Infrastructure Committee chair failed, and Gibbs said he and the vice chair would meet to recommend a chair for next month.

### Ordinance 27-26 - Battery Energy Storage Systems (BESS) Amendment
Staff planner Shad Harvey presented the ordinance as strengthening existing Chapter 17 BESS protections. Supervisor Dickinson raised the question of whether the 300-foot residential setback was adequate given ember travel in fires, and staff recommended 500 feet as a defensible middle ground. Supervisor Sundoski moved to refer the ordinance back to the Environmental Resources Committee (ERC) citing concerns about missing provisions on waterways, decommissioning, and liability; this motion was seconded by Supervisor Hapa but failed on a vote. Supervisor Dickinson then moved to amend the setback from 300 to 500 feet, and Supervisor Sundoski moved a further amendment to extend that setback to all structures, not just residences. Both amendments passed. The ordinance as twice-amended was then approved, though not unanimously. Staff clarified that standalone BESS under county zoning require conditional use permits reviewed by the Board of Adjustment, and system owners are required to provide and replace specialized firefighting equipment for first responders.

### Ordinance 28-26 - Moratorium on Data Centers
Motion by Supervisor Drebeck, seconded by Supervisor Conway. Chair Gibbs noted the county currently has no Chapter 17 provisions for data centers, and the moratorium of up to 12 months gives staff time to develop an ordinance. No significant debate was recorded. The ordinance passed but was not unanimous.

### Ordinance 29-26 - Large Energy Generating Facilities Amendment
This novel ordinance — described by outside counsel Rebecca Roker as the first of its kind in Wisconsin — would require developers to submit engineering plans to the county 60 days before filing a CPCN application with the PSC, giving the county an early engagement opportunity. Supervisor Leur expressed concern about vague, unmeasurable language in sections 3 and 4 regarding 'undue adverse impact' and 'unreasonably interfere.' Supervisor Sundoski moved to refer back to ERC for consideration of a moratorium; that motion failed. Supervisor Dickinson then moved to amend the ordinance to require the county to share received engineering plan information with all municipalities in Marathon County, seconded by Supervisor Arstead; that amendment passed unanimously. The ordinance as amended was then approved, though not unanimously. Supervisor Gao cautioned that vague moratorium language would be dismissed by the PSC.

### Resolution 49-26 (E18) - Phase 3 Construction of New Highway Facility
Supervisor Krebs gave a lengthy statement opposing the project on fiscal grounds, comparing the potential $55-60 million price tag unfavorably to a fire station, police department, and DPW garage combined, and calling for a public referendum. Supervisors Robinson, Dickinson, Griner, Ness, and Vice Chair Boots defended the resolution, emphasizing it only authorizes staff to release bid documents to obtain a guaranteed maximum price — not to break ground — and that construction costs continue to rise annually. Concerns were raised about coal dust from the adjacent WPS plant affecting employee health; staff noted HVAC filtration is designed to address particulate matter and WPS is expected to transition from coal to natural gas by the early 2030s. Administrator Leonard confirmed the resolution accomplishes two things: authorizing bid release and approving a general financial plan using previously issued debt to reduce new borrowing needs. The vote outcome was not explicitly stated in the transcript before the recording ended.

No public comment was offered.

## Action Items
- Chair Gibbs and Vice Chair to meet and recommend a new Infrastructure Committee chair for next month's meeting
- Rules Review Committee to consider establishing clearer protocol for filling committee chair vacancies
- BESS ordinance (27-26) adopted with 500-foot setback from all structures; staff to implement amended Chapter 17 provisions
- Data center moratorium (Ordinance 28-26) adopted; staff to develop a data center zoning ordinance within 12 months
- Large energy generating facilities ordinance (29-26) adopted as amended; staff to share engineering plan information with all county municipalities upon receipt
- Staff directed to release bid documents for Phase 3 highway facility construction and return with guaranteed maximum price for board consideration
- Staff to prepare operational cost comparison between current and proposed highway facilities for HR Finance and Property Committee review in October
- Administrator Leonard to look into air quality data near proposed highway facility site related to WPS coal dust drift
