# Wausau Human Resources Committee Meeting

**Organization:** City of Wausau  
**Source:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=iaAmUPsyr5U  
**Summarized:** 2026-08-30 04:34 UTC

## Meeting Overview
The Wausau Human Resources Committee approved a new collective bargaining agreement with the Amalgamated Transit Union Local 1168 and narrowly approved a firefighter-paramedic apprenticeship partnership with North Central Technical College (NTC) on a 3-2 vote, with an amendment requiring language about grant expiration in contracts. The committee also held an extensive discussion on city overtime practices across the police, fire, and DPW departments, revealing systemic challenges with data reporting and HR oversight. The meeting concluded with a farewell acknowledgment for outgoing HR Director Lisa Noak, whose last day is July 31st.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of Minutes of Previous Meetings
The chair called for a motion to accept the minutes. A motion was made by Alder Kilian and seconded by Alder Wowski. The motion passed, with the chair noting approval after an initial procedural misstep where she forgot to call for the vote.

### Bargaining Agreement Between City of Wausau and Local 1168 Amalgamated Transit Union AFL-CIO
HR Director Lisa Noak presented the tentative agreement reached after three bargaining sessions, describing it as a smooth and respectful process. Key terms include a 2-year agreement (July 1, 2026 – June 30, 2028), removal of the perfect attendance leave (PAL) program, uniform concessions, a revised safety shoe provision, and a compensation schedule of 2% followed by two 1.5% increases at six-month intervals, totaling a $60,021 financial impact already earmarked in the budget. Alder Wowski asked about sticking points; Noak said the city's primary concern was ensuring compensation was consistent with inflation and comparable to non-represented employees, noting Wausau led the state in compensation after the previous CBA. The motion to approve was made and passed.

### Request for Approval of Firefighter Paramedic Apprenticeship Partnership with NTC
Fire Chief Cop presented the program, which had been sent back from a prior meeting for additional information. Alder Kilian raised concerns about workers' comp and FMLA liability, noting the program was previously described as budget-neutral, and said she was 'really debating' whether she could support it given tight budgets. Alder Wowski raised questions about clawback provisions if apprentices drop out early, grant money expiration scenarios, and what would happen to apprentices if federal funding disappeared mid-program. Brad Gas from NTC clarified that the program has DWD approval, apprentices earn credentials each semester so they would not be left without skills, and if grant money evaporated the city would terminate apprentices as LTEs rather than absorb costs. A motion was made and seconded on the main item, and then an amendment was introduced by Alder Wowski to add language to both the MOU and individual apprentice contracts specifying that the city would terminate apprenticeships if grant funding expires. The amendment passed 5-0 and the main motion as amended passed 3-2, with Alders Wowski, Martins, and Kilian in favor and Alders Larson and Churnney opposed.

### Discussion of City Overtime – Police Department
Police Chief Matt Barnes delivered a detailed presentation on police overtime, identifying 'late call' (1,132 hours, 12.29% of overtime in 2024) and 'special event' (1,039 hours, 11.29%) as the top two drivers. Barnes explained that late calls are largely uncontrollable — officers cannot abandon investigations mid-stream — and that special events are approved by the council. He noted that 1,311 hours of 2024 overtime and 1,217 hours of 2025 overtime were 100% reimbursed to the city. In response to Alder Wowski's questions, Barnes explained that paying overtime is more cost-effective than hiring additional staff: employing four additional officers would cost roughly $600,000 annually in salary and benefits, while the department spent between $400,000–$450,000 in overtime. He explained the seniority-based overtime offer system, pension-banking incentives near retirement, and contractual rules ensuring minimum rest between shifts. Alder Kilian raised the issue of cost-sharing for events like the Wisconsin Valley Fair hosted at county-owned Marathon Park. Alder Wowski raised the need for clearer public reporting showing how much overtime is reimbursed versus borne by taxpayers. HR Director Noak and Finance Director Maryanne discussed a $70,000 discrepancy in overtime figures from open records requests, attributing it to mismatched tracking systems between Intellitime and Workday, and missing pay codes. Noak acknowledged she has had no visibility into payroll since joining a year ago, which she called unusual based on her prior experience.

### Discussion of City Overtime – Fire Department
Fire Chief Cop noted that fire overtime drivers mirror the police department's — primarily FMLA and sick coverage for a young department. He explained that the previous internal apprenticeship program contributed to overtime because apprentices counted against payroll but not toward staffing minimums, requiring shift coverage. He described cost-saving measures including in-house training, reduced outside schooling, and creative revenue generation through increased fee schedules for ambulance, reinspections, lockouts, and standbys. He noted the department brings in $190,000–$202,000 in annual revenue, partially offsetting overtime costs.

### Discussion of City Overtime – DPW
DPW Director Lynman briefly presented, noting his department is non-union with six divisions, four of which drive most overtime. Winter maintenance (streets/snow removal and plowing) is the largest driver and weather-dependent. He emphasized that contracting out construction inspection is significantly more expensive than staff overtime — one downtown project cost nearly $150,000 in contracted inspection alone — making overtime the fiscally responsible choice. The department typically comes in under its overtime budget. Lynman offered to answer further questions at a later time given the meeting's time constraints.

### Farewell Remarks for HR Director Lisa Noak
The chair offered farewell remarks for HR Director Lisa Noak, whose last day is July 31st. The chair highlighted Noak's commitment to staff, her work updating the employee handbook, her advocacy for summer hours and extended customer service availability, and her efforts to build departmental relationships. The committee applauded Noak.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. The chair asked three times and received no response.
