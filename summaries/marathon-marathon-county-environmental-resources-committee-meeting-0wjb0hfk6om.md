## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Environmental Resources Committee met on June 30, 2026, approving three rezoning petitions, a stormwater memorandum of agreement renewal, a town zoning code amendment, and directing staff to move forward on battery energy storage system ordinance amendments and farmland preservation ordinance updates. The committee also received a year-to-date budget briefing and a presentation on the 2027 budget development process. Key decisions included advancing battery energy storage and data center moratorium amendments toward an August 4 public hearing.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment
Colin Kishop, a property owner on Lake Dubay in the Township of Nolton and a Portage County resident, spoke online about worsening algae conditions on Lake Dubay. He said that when he purchased his cottage in 2017 there was no algae problem, but by 2022 algae began appearing in August, and in 2026 algae was present on his shoreline before the Fourth of July. He expressed concern that algae is spreading downstream through the plover river corridor into Stevens Point and beyond, and suggested the source may be tributaries of the Plover River. No committee action was taken on this comment.

### Approval of June 2026 Committee Minutes
Supervisor Drebeck moved to approve the June 2026 minutes, seconded by Supervisor Shoneborn. The motion carried unanimously with no discussion.

### Item 5-A1: Rezoning - Mitch Gums, Farmland Preservation to Low Density Residential, Town of Hull
Staff member Garrett Pogle presented the staff report, noting the rezoning meets all zoning district standards and is consistent with the town's comprehensive plan. The Town of Hull recommended approval on May 11, 2026. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Drebeck moved to approve based on findings of fact, staff report, conclusions of law, and town recommendation; Supervisor Kurt seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

### Item 5-A2: Rezoning - Sandra Tremling, General Agriculture to Rural Estate and Rural Residential, Town of Spencer
Garrett Pogle presented the staff report indicating the rezoning meets all standards and is consistent with the town's comprehensive plan. The Town of Spencer recommended approval on May 11, 2026. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Shoneborn moved to approve; Supervisor Kurt seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

### Item 5-A3: Rezoning - Rome Properties LLC, Farmland Preservation to General Agriculture, Town of McMillan
Garrett Pogle presented the staff report. A committee member asked about the difference between Farmland Preservation and General Agriculture zoning; Pogle explained FP requires a 35-acre minimum and allows state tax credits, while GA has a 10-acre minimum, and the petitioner sought the change to allow family members to build a home on a smaller parcel. Petitioner David Falcoski was sworn in online but offered no additional testimony. No public testimony was offered. Supervisor Shoneborn moved to approve; Supervisor Drebeck seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

### Item 5-B1: Town of Day Zoning Code Amendment - Minimum Side Yard Setback
Staff explained that the Town of Day, which has its own zoning code, proposed reducing the minimum side yard setback from 30 feet to 7 feet. The town conducted its own public hearing process and forwarded the amendment for ERC approval as required by county procedure. Supervisor Drebeck moved to accept the resolution from the Town of Day; Supervisor Kurt seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

### Item 5-C1: Stormwater MOA with Municipalities for MS4 Compliance
Director Lori McKimmons explained that Marathon County renews its Municipal Separate Storm Sewer System (MS4) memorandum of agreement with surrounding municipalities every five years to maintain compliance with its stormwater permit. Corporation Counsel confirmed ERC approval is sufficient and county board action is not required. Supervisor Drebeck moved to approve the North Central Wisconsin Stormwater Management Coalition MOA; Supervisor Kurt seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

### Item 6-A1: Data Center Moratorium and Energy Facility Amendments Update
Staff reported that both the data center moratorium and the large energy generating facilities ordinance have been sent to towns for comment. Staff noted one recommended change to the data center moratorium: removing the zoning map amendment provision from covered actions, as state statute prohibits counties from including rezones in a development moratorium. Staff confirmed this was reviewed with Corporation Counsel and does not substantively alter the moratorium. A public hearing is scheduled for August 4, 2026, when town comments will be presented.

### Item 6-A2: Year-to-Date Budget Report for CPZ, PRF, and SWD
Finance staff presented the year-to-date budget reports through March 2026 for the CPZ, Parks/Forestry, and Solid Waste departments. Staff noted payroll for all three departments is within the expected 23.75% benchmark. Notable items included a one-time $73,252 office supplies charge in CPZ for the Ascent land records program (to be reallocated via budget modification) and a first-quarter 85.21 transportation program payment. No concerns were raised; the committee received the report with no action taken.

### Item 6-A3: 2027 Budget Development Discussion
County Administrator [inaudible - name not clearly stated] and HR/Finance/Property Committee Chair Robinson presented the budget development framework, urging committee members to engage early on three key policy questions: what programs and services to deliver, at what service level, and at what cost. The administrator emphasized that fees must keep pace with rising service costs or the county will face service reductions or increased levy subsidies. A committee member, identified as Mara or a farm representative, raised concerns about a $6 fee related to septic system inspections, stating it was unfair to rural residents. The administrator noted staff will handle delivery decisions but policy direction must come from elected officials.

### Item 7-A: Battery Energy Storage Systems Ordinance Amendments
Staff presented proposed amendments to Marathon County's existing battery energy storage ordinance, including new requirements for risk mitigation analysis, emergency response plans approved by the local fire chief and Marathon County Emergency Management, minimum fence heights, annual plan reviews, first responder training and equipment provided by developers, developer liability for emergency event costs, continuous remote monitoring, and enhanced decommissioning language. Committee members raised concerns about who pays for contaminated water cleanup after a battery fire, evacuation costs for nearby residents, and how spill remediation costs would be quantified. Staff acknowledged the financial assurance provisions cover decommissioning and spill cleanup but noted evacuation reimbursement is not currently included in the ordinance. Supervisor Drebeck moved to advance the amendments; Supervisor Shoneborn seconded. Motion carried unanimously, directing staff to proceed toward a public hearing at the August 4, 2026 meeting.

### Item 7-B: Farmland Preservation Ordinance Updates
Staff explained the county's farmland preservation ordinance must be recertified by DATCAP by January 1, 2027 in order for property owners to continue claiming farmland preservation tax credits. Proposed amendments include removing the farm consolidation pathway (which a majority of surveyed county-zoned towns supported and which DATCAP also required to be corrected), adding language to address existing pre-existing farm consolidations, allowing private battery energy storage systems (under 1 megawatt, on-site only) in the Farm Preservation District, and general cleanup of use table errors. Supervisor Anson moved to approve sending the amendments to DATCAP and to the towns for comment; Supervisor Shoneborn seconded. Motion carried unanimously.

## Public Comment
One online speaker, Colin Kishop of the Township of Nolton (a Portage County resident and Lake Dubay cottage owner since 2017), testified about worsening algae conditions on Lake Dubay. He stated algae problems began around 2022 and have progressively worsened, with algae now appearing on his shoreline before the Fourth of July 2026. He expressed concern that algae is spreading downstream through the Plover River into Stevens Point and beyond, and suggested the source may be tributaries of the old Plover River. He said he was advised by someone in the conservation department to bring the issue to the committee.
