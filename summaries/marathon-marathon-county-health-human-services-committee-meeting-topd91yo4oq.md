## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Health & Human Services Committee met on what appears to be a June or July 2026 session, approving two significant items: authorization to apply for a federal HUD lead reduction grant of up to $7 million and a budget amendment implementing new SNAP quality assurance staffing requirements under 2025 Wisconsin Act 116. The committee also received a 2027 budget development overview and first-quarter year-to-date financial reports, with notable discussion about expanding the Crisis Assessment Response Team (CART) program to evening and weekend hours.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of June 3rd, 2026 Meeting Minutes
Minutes were approved without discussion via voice vote. No objections were noted.

### 5A1: Request to Apply for Federal HUD Lead Reduction Grant
Kate Floric of the Health Department presented on a HUD Lead Hazard Reduction Production Grant of up to $7 million over three years, which Marathon County would apply for independently given its larger geographic coverage compared to the City of Wausau's existing capacity-building grant. Floric noted approximately 70% of county homes and 80% of Wausau homes were built before 1978, creating significant lead hazard exposure risk, and estimated the grant could remediate 30–45 homes over three years. Supervisor Rosenberg enthusiastically supported the motion, citing the permanent nature of childhood lead poisoning and praising the county-city partnership; Supervisor Krammes asked about homeowner costs (none required) and the 10% match requirement, which staff said would not be problematic. The committee voted to authorize the application.

### 5A2: 2027 Budget Development and Policy Recommendations
HR Finance and Property Committee Chair Robinson and County Administrator Leonard presented the budget development framework, emphasizing three core questions: what programs to deliver, at what service level, and at what cost. Significant discussion centered on expanding the CART (Crisis Assessment Response Team) program to evening and weekend hours; Supervisor Krammes, a retired law enforcement officer, stated he would only support expansion if hours were extended beyond weekdays and early evenings, noting he never once received a CART response during his patrol career. Chair Robinson highlighted SNAP administrative concerns under federal changes and praised the health department's fee structure as a model. Administrator Leonard noted the county's forensic science center and POWTS monitoring program as recent examples of improved fee-for-service cost recovery. No vote was taken on budget policy at this meeting.

### 5B1: Budget Amendment for 2025 Wisconsin Act 116 SNAP Quality Assurance Mandate
Administrator Leonard presented a budget amendment and staffing plan required by both federal HR1 legislation and the resulting 2025 Wisconsin Act 116, which mandated new quality assurance requirements for SNAP administration. Marathon County, as lead county of a four-county consortium (Marathon, Portage, Oneida, and Langlade), must create between five and six new quality assurance positions funded entirely by $539,857 in state appropriations. Leonard emphasized positions would be hired from experienced consortium staff and added incrementally, and that positions are tied to state funding — if funding ends, positions end. Supervisor Crivello asked about sunset provisions and future state budget approval; staff confirmed the positions are funded through 2026 and 2027 under the current contract addendum. The committee approved the budget amendment and staffing plan.

### 6A: Year-to-Date Budget Report (Q1 2026)
Finance staff member Sam presented first-quarter 2026 budget reports for Social Improvements, Health, and Veterans departments. Staff reported no significant concerns: payroll spending rates were on track given that grants funds are drawn first; a noted overage in special services for Social Improvements reflects consortium lead pass-through expenditures with a correlating revenue offset, to be corrected via budget amendment. Health and Veterans departments were both described as on track with no issues flagged.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered.
