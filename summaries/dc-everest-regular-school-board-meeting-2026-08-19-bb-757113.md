## Meeting Overview
The DC Everest School Board held its regular August meeting, approving the 10-year capital plan and employee handbook, receiving a summer school report, and hearing a public comment raising concerns about seclusion room practices at Evergreen Elementary. Board member Larry Schaefer received a commendation letter from CISA 9 administrator Al Betri recognizing his four years of service on the CISA 9 Board of Control.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment – Seclusion Room at Evergreen Elementary
Mary Soulheim, a district resident and parent of students at Evergreen Elementary, addressed the board about behavioral intervention practices. She stated the district was found in direct violation of Wisconsin Statute 118.305 for maintaining a seclusion room with a locked door and hazards at Evergreen Elementary during the 2024-2025 school year. She described the room as a former storage closet measuring just over 4 by 6 feet, and argued that retrofitting it with padding and training staff to use it for seclusion would be traumatizing to students and shift school culture away from proactive mental health support. She urged the board to halt the rollout of the plan and direct administration to conduct a review of legal and psychological risks, proposing the topic be brought back in September.

### CISA 9 Report and Commendation of Larry Schaefer
Larry Schaefer reported on the CISA 9 annual meeting, noting that Gary Moon from North Lakeland was elected as the large-district representative, ending Schaefer's tenure on the CISA 9 Board of Control after four years. Board President Shannon Grabko read aloud a commendation letter from CISA 9 administrator Al Betri praising Schaefer's service, leadership, and professionalism. Schaefer encouraged any board member interested in attending future CISA 9 annual meetings or serving on the board of control to do so, describing it as a valuable perspective on upcoming educational issues.

### Superintendent Report – Facility Study, Staff PD, AI Bot, and Law Enforcement Training
Superintendent Dr. Knight reported that the community survey on the facility study is being finalized, with staff receiving it September 14 and community members receiving it by USPS the week of September 21; a board workshop to review results is expected in November. Officer Matt Loveless described a four-day joint critical incident rescue task force training held at the middle school July 27-30, involving approximately 30 Mountain Bay Metro Police officers and 20 Riverside Fire District personnel, using student volunteers for realistic scenarios. Dr. Knight also reported that after a demo, the district decided not to implement a $4,000 AI chatbot from Final Site this year due to cost, quality concerns, and pending ADA compliance rules effective April 2026.

### Summer School Report
Mike Rayther and Nikki Matthews presented the summer learning report in Rayther's first year leading the program. Highlights included an Engineering Tomorrow partnership with Greenheck on sound mitigation, strength and conditioning programming with CTI, a patriotic musical performance called 'America 250' involving about 70 students, the Hmong enrichment program co-run with Wausau School District and CESA, new courses including podcasting, sign language ('Flying Fingers'), and an art course for first and second graders, and the Fuel for the Weekend food program serving 127 families. Board members praised the program and asked about challenges and staff retention; Rayther said returning staff significantly outnumbered new staff this summer.

### 10-Year Capital Plan Annual Approval
Dr. Strike presented the annual 10-year capital plan, noting it is required because the district holds a Fund 46 capital trust fund. She highlighted that some items are marked 'hold for facility study' pending the outcome of the ongoing facility planning process, and that furniture replacement has become an increasing need. The board approved the plan without discussion.

### Employee Handbook
Dr. Strike presented the updated employee handbook for the upcoming school year, noting five themes of changes compiled by the talent and culture department, including Christy Eser and Sarah Trimmer. She noted most changes had been previously presented to the board earlier in the year and were being formalized through the handbook process. The board approved the handbook without discussion.

## Public Comment
One speaker offered public comment. Mary Soulheim, of 2580 Sussex Place, a district resident and parent of students including one at Evergreen Elementary, raised concerns about the district's use of a seclusion room at Evergreen Elementary, alleging a violation of Wisconsin Statute 118.305. She described the room as a repurposed 4-by-6-foot storage closet and urged the board to halt plans to formally train staff to use it, calling for an administrative review of legal and psychological risks to be brought back to the board in September. A second person, Amy Evelyn, was called to the podium but no remarks from her were captured in the transcript.
