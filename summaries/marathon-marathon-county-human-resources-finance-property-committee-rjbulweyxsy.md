## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Human Resources, Finance & Property Committee met to address several significant items including a tattoo convention licensing fee restructure, 2027 budget development discussions, position reclassifications, and a resolution to proceed with Phase 3 construction planning for a new county highway facility. The committee approved a modified health department fee schedule for body art establishments, approved a slate of position changes and reclassifications for the 2027 budget, authorized Phase 3 highway facility planning, and approved July claims totaling approximately $13.7 million.

## Key Discussions
### Public Comment — Tattoo Convention Licensing
Three speakers addressed the committee regarding agenda item 6B6. Dave Bruo, representing the Village of Rothschild as tourism marketing director, outlined the economic impact of the Cedar Creek Ink Fest tattoo convention, estimating approximately $497,000 in total economic impact and $1,500 in county tax revenue based on 3,000 attendees. Mark Hannenburg, owner of Marked Up Tattoos and holder of one of the 32 permanent body art establishment licenses in Marathon County, supported the fee restructure concept but raised concerns about fairness of mid-year fee changes affecting permanent establishments that already renewed. Brandy Cannenburgg, assisting the event organizers, noted that under the current interpretation, 46 to 60 separate licenses would be required at a cost of $9,568 to $18,480, and argued a single event-level license may be legally correct under existing statute, noting Sauk County licensed the same organizer's convention as a single establishment.

### 6B6 — Health Department Fee Schedule Modification for Tattoo and Piercing Licenses
Administrator Leonard and Health Department Director Kate Floric explained the proposed change would restructure establishment licensing fees on a tiered, practitioner-based model — one license covering up to six practitioners — for both permanent and temporary establishments, replacing the current per-establishment interpretation. Floric noted the change would actually represent a slight decrease (to approximately $208) for existing permanent establishments and would result in an estimated net levy impact of negative $400 in a normal year. Vice Chair [inaudible] confirmed that the new fee structure would be cheaper for the Cedar Creek Ink Fest than the current interpretation. The committee voted to approve the modified fee schedule, which will proceed to the full county board.

### 6A1 — 2027 Budget Development and Policy Recommendations
Administrator Leonard presented updates on budget development, noting departments are reviewing fees against a board-directed 3% increase benchmark. Preliminary data showed equalized valuation grew 6.25% and net new construction at 1.545%, yielding approximately $800,000 in new levy capacity. Supervisor [inaudible, likely Robinson] pressed on whether the county must spend the new levy capacity, and Administrator Leonard explained that given existing debt obligations and rising costs, not maximizing the operating levy would be fiscally unsound. Supervisor [inaudible] noted the tax rate has dropped from $6.87 per $1,000 in 2006 to approximately $3.45 due to levy limits outpacing equalized value growth, and Vice Chair P expressed preference for spending only on existing needs rather than new programs.

### 6A2 — Position Requests and Reclassifications for 2027 Budget
Administrator Leonard presented a comprehensive list of position changes across multiple departments, including the DA's office (abolish one restitution specialist, create one victim witness specialist and 0.5 administrative specialist), Corrections (eliminate administrative position, create corrections officer), Clerk of Courts (reclass to administrative specialist), Medical Examiner (abolish vacant deputy chief physician position, create senior administrative specialist), Administration (reclass driven by tax foreclosure duties), Health Department (abolish lab tech, create sanitarian; restructure business manager to senior admin plus new finance accountant), Social Services (child support realignment and six new CLTS positions fully funded by Medicaid), and Facilities/Capital Management (abolish maintenance tech, create maintenance specialist). Supervisor Jang expressed hesitation about approving all positions at once without knowing which had already been vetted, and Administrator Leonard clarified that all items except the Facilities/Capital Management position and the Administration reclass (going to Executive the next day) had already been approved by standing committees, several unanimously. Vice Chair Boots, attending remotely via WebEx, confirmed Health and Human Services approved the relevant items 7-0. Chair Gibbs moved to approve the position changes for the 2027 budget planning process, contingent on executive committee approving the administration reclass.

### July Claims and Question Costs
The committee considered July claims totaling approximately $13,685,665. No substantive discussion occurred beyond a quip about health insurance and asphalt costs. The motion to approve passed unanimously.

### Claims Disallowance — Schillinger
Corporation Counsel explained the statutory claims process, noting the county's insurance company recommended disallowing the Schillinger claim. The committee voted to disallow the claim; notice will be sent by certified mail.

### 6B1 — Resolution Authorizing Phase 3 Highway Facility Construction Planning
Facilities Director Chris [inaudible] summarized design progress, including completed zoning change in the Village of Kronenwetter, ongoing site plan approval, preliminary site clearing and home demolitions, and a planned September bidding process to generate a guaranteed maximum price (GMP). Administrator Leonard and Finance Director Sam [inaudible] presented two financing scenarios: issuing approximately $20.3 million in new general obligation debt (at current ~4% rates costing roughly $9.5 million in interest) versus using accumulated North Central Healthcare revenues by transitioning existing Lake View campus debt to the debt levy over time, potentially avoiding a new large bond issuance and saving an estimated $4 million in interest. The strategy would generate cumulative funds sufficient to cover a $20–25 million project gap by approximately 2030–2033 depending on equalized value growth. The resolution does not commit funding but authorizes continued design finalization and bidding, with a final project authorization presentation expected by the October 15 county board education meeting.

## Public Comment
Three speakers offered public comment, all related to the tattoo convention licensing fee item. Dave Bruo (167949 Luchwagger Drive, Ringle), representing the Village of Rothschild as tourism marketing director, presented an economic impact analysis projecting approximately $497,000 in total economic impact from the Cedar Creek Ink Fest tattoo convention. Mark Hannenburg, owner of Marked Up Tattoos in Marathon County, supported restructuring fees but asked that any changes to permanent establishment fees take effect at each business's next renewal rather than mid-year. Brandy Cannenburgg, assisting event organizers, described the current fee structure as potentially requiring 46–60 licenses costing up to $18,480 for a three-day event, and noted the matter remains legally unresolved in her view, while prioritizing a clear licensing structure before the October 16–18 event.
