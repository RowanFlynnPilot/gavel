## Meeting Overview
The Marathon County Infrastructure Committee met to approve several infrastructure and budget items, including a new four-way stop at County Trunk X and Tesh Lane/Turbani Drive, a resolution supporting changes to state trucking restrictions on Business 51, an amendment to the 2026 budget for LRIP funding, and authorization to proceed with design finalization and bidding for a new Marathon County highway shop. The highway shop financing discussion was particularly significant, with staff presenting a strategy to use existing low-interest debt and North Central Healthcare revenues to minimize new borrowing costs to taxpayers on what is currently estimated as a roughly $50 million project.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of June/July 2, 2026 Meeting Minutes
Chair Gibbs called for a motion to approve the minutes. A motion was made by Gregor and seconded by Sterber; Gibbs initially missed Coyle's involvement and apologized. The motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

### 2027 Budget Development - Fee Schedule and New Positions
Staff (identified as Mr. Leonard, the county administrator) reported that departments have been circulated the existing fee schedule following the board's direction to apply a standard 3% fee increase, with exceptions being reviewed for statutory limits, areas where costs have not risen, and market-sensitive services like solid waste. Leonard also reported no new position requests or reclassifications were submitted for departments under the Infrastructure Committee's jurisdiction. Chair Gibbs noted that three resolutions from the ERC committee may bring future cost and staffing implications and asked that those be considered during the budget process.

### Four-Way Stop at County Trunk X / Tesh Lane / Turbani Drive
Highway Commissioner Kevin presented a traffic study by EMCS showing the intersection met crash warrant criteria (exceeding five crashes in a one-month period), with a majority being right-angle crashes, which are among the most severe. Volume warrants were close to being met, and a speed study was expected to confirm the reduced threshold would apply at the 35 mph posted limit. The Village of Rothschild had previously recommended against stopping major traffic on Double X but lacked crash data at the time; the Village of Kronenwetter had not yet responded. Kevin recommended converting to a four-way stop. Supervisor Kelly moved to recommend the county board modify Section 7.04(6) of the Marathon County Code of Ordinances to create a four-way stop; Chair Gibbs amended the motion to include flashing stop signs on County Trunk X at the committee's request. The motion carried unanimously.

### Resolution Supporting Modification of Trans 276.065 Trucking Restrictions on Business 51
Kevin explained that the existing state restriction prohibiting trucks over 65 feet on Business 51 from I-39 to US-51 near Highway K is unenforced and inconsistent with actual use by businesses like Domar Tile using standard 53-foot semi-trailers. Supervisor Coyle moved to recommend the county board support a resolution encouraging the state to modify Trans 276 to allow vehicles exceeding 65 feet on Business 51 from the Rothschild exit up to and including the Highway 29 exit, expanding the original scope. Supervisor Griner questioned whether the limit should extend further north to I-39/51, but Kevin noted uncertainty about downtown Wausau street capacity. The motion was seconded by Griner and carried unanimously.

### Amendment to 2026 Budget for LRIP Funding - Highway E
Kevin explained that an unusual pavement failure on Highway E north of Marshfield — where the lower asphalt layer stripped and failed rapidly — forced the project into the 2026 paving program ahead of schedule. LRIP funds from future years were applied to offset costs, but because the decision was made after the budget cycle, those revenues need to be added to the current year budget. Supervisor She moved to approve the budget amendment; Supervisor Griner seconded. The motion carried unanimously on a voice vote.

### Marathon County Highway Shop Construction - Funding Mechanism and Project Authorization
Administrator Leonard and Finance Director Sam Fensky presented a detailed financing strategy for the new highway shop, estimated at approximately $50 million (with the actual figure expected to differ). The county has $30 million in highway fund reserves designated for the project. Staff proposed avoiding new long-term debt by instead transitioning existing low-interest debt (issued 2019-2022 at roughly 2% for Lake View campus renovations) currently serviced by North Central Healthcare revenues onto the general debt levy, and redirecting those North Central revenues (ramping from $3 million to $4.1 million annually) to fund the highway shop — saving an estimated $25 million in interest compared to issuing new debt at current 4-5% rates. Chris presented project design progress, noting the design is 90-100% complete, coordinated burns of demolished homes on the Kronenwetter site have been completed with the fire department, and bids are targeted for September with a guaranteed maximum price and final county board approval expected by mid-October. Construction would begin in earnest in spring 2027 due to 10-12 month lead times for pre-cast concrete panels. Geothermal HVAC and enhanced insulation were cited as energy efficiency strategies. Supervisor Griner moved to authorize staff to proceed with phase three construction and finalize construction documents for bidding; Supervisor Coyle seconded. The motion carried unanimously.

### NOTIP Program 2027-2030 Chapter Five - MPO Projects Discussion
Chair Gibbs walked through projects in the Metropolitan Planning Organization's long-range plan, including the Evergreen-Decatur Wisconsin River crossing in Wausau, the Bridge Street bridge widening, County Trunk Highway O river crossing to Highway NN, the Oman bridge in Schofield over the Eau Claire River, Military Road Wisconsin River crossing, Gardner Park Road in Kronenwetter, County Trunk Highway X to Sandy Lane in Weston, the Kowalski Road interchange, and the Ryan Street interchange at Highway 29 in Weston. Supervisor Robertson cautioned that many of these are long-standing wish-list items requiring community-led studies and environmental review before advancing, citing the Kowalski Road interchange as an example that keeps being listed without completing required preliminary steps. Kevin emphasized that purpose-and-need justification and environmental impacts — especially wetlands and floodplains along the Wisconsin River — are major hurdles for any new crossings. Administrator Leonard suggested inviting MPO planner Lori Kimmons to a future meeting to provide an overview as those discussions develop, which the committee agreed was a good idea.

### Bug Tussle Broadband - Joint Meeting Announcement
Supervisor Robertson noted that at the last executive committee meeting, a discussion was held about Bug Tussle's failure to complete the committed southern fiber loop. A joint meeting between the Infrastructure Committee and the HR, Finance and Property Committee will be scheduled to evaluate next steps regarding the county's agreement with Bug Tussle.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. Chair Gibbs asked twice if anyone was present for public comment and, seeing none, proceeded to the agenda.
