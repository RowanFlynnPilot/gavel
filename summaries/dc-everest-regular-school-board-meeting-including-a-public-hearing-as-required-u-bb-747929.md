## Meeting Overview
The DC Everest School Board held its regular June meeting, highlighted by a public hearing and subsequent board approval of a waiver request to DPI for Riverside Elementary's 12.5 hours of lost instructional time due to the April 17, 2026 tornado. The board also approved a staff compensation plan, year-end spending directives, preliminary budget for 2026-27, and out-of-state trip requests, then adjourned into closed session.

## Key Discussions
### Public Hearing - Riverside Elementary Instructional Minutes Waiver
The board held the statutorily required public hearing under Wis. Stat. 118.38(1)(b) regarding a waiver request to DPI for Riverside Elementary's lost instructional time following the April 17, 2026 tornado. No members of the public offered comment at the hearing. The board subsequently passed the formal resolution directing the superintendent to apply to DPI for the waiver of 12.5 lost instructional hours by a 6-0 roll call vote (Yee Leng Xiong was absent).

### Superintendent's Report
Superintendent Dr. [last name inaudible from transcript] discussed several topics including the district's participation in the Greater Wausau Prosperity Project community dashboard comparing Wausau-area strengths and challenges to similar communities, noting food insecurity, migration, and lack of young entrepreneurs as challenges while startup growth rates and PK-16 education ranked as strengths. He also updated the board on the facility study process, noting a community survey is planned for late September or October focused primarily on the junior high and pool questions. The district's youth apprenticeship program was highlighted as moving in-house after receiving DPI grant approval, having grown to 80 apprentices with 39 employers.

### Mission Moment - Weston Elementary Soaring into Summer Aviation Event
Fifth grade teacher Lauren Dasher and former kindergarten teacher Katie Winsky presented on the Weston Elementary 'Soaring into Summer' event, which took 500 students over three days to the Wausau Airport for hands-on aviation learning including airplane building with high school engineering students, simulator use, small plane tours, and Q&A with flight instructors. The event was funded by the Education Foundation and coordinated with the Wausau Airport's John [inaudible] and Aaron Huffman's high school Engineering Tomorrow class. Board members praised the community collaboration and connection to career pathway development.

### Annual Required Student Data Reports / Crime Statistics
Director Jeff [inaudible] explained the consolidated student data reports, noting that seclusion and restraint numbers involving fewer than five incidents are withheld as personally identifiable, and that 14 students (less than 0.25% of enrollment) experienced seclusion or restraint during the year. The board approved the 2025-2026 DC Everest Crime Statistics on School Property Report, the only item in the packet requiring formal board action, on a voice vote.

### Education for Employment ACP Report and Youth Apprenticeship Presentation
Career and Technical Education Director Aaron Huffman presented the annual Academic and Career Planning report, highlighting 80 youth apprentices placed with 39 employers this year, a record exceeding 200 industry-recognized credentials earned by the senior class, and a drone competition where DC Everest teams took first and second place out of 28 schools. Student Dakota shared his experience pivoting from welding at J&D to electrical and machine control work at Imperial, illustrating the program's goal of allowing students to explore and modify career paths. The presentation was informational only and required no board action.

### Junior High and IDEA Out-of-State Trip Approvals
The board approved out-of-state trips for the junior high to Washington, D.C. and Babi, Minnesota, and for the IDEA program's senior trip to Minneapolis, Minnesota. A brief exchange occurred in which a board member asked whether the language for the junior high trip destination was correctly stated this year, referencing an error in a prior year where Spain and France were listed incorrectly. Administration indicated the language was correct. The motion passed on a voice vote.

### Staff Compensation Plan
Dr. [inaudible] presented the comprehensive staff compensation plan for 2026-2027, explaining it consolidates all compensation information in one document. Most raises had already been approved; this action addressed remaining miscellaneous items including increased substitute wages, minor rate and title adjustments for part-time GTCC staff, and the addition of a girls wrestling coach position. The board approved the plan on a unanimous voice vote.

### 2026-2027 Budget Update and Year-End Spending
The district's finance director presented the preliminary 2026-27 budget, noting the current year is projected to end with a surplus of $1.5 to $2 million, largely from salary and fringe savings and conservative revenue estimates. The 2026-27 budget is currently balanced in Funds 10 and 27. A significant concern raised was health insurance, with a 22% renewal increase assumed due to the highest claims loss ratio in district history. The board approved year-end spending authority directing administration to use surplus funds to pay back a $1,448,000 unfunded liability in Fund 73 (staff retirement benefit catch-up contributions approved last June) with remaining funds deposited into Fund 46 by July 30, 2026. The board also approved authority to spend from the preliminary budget until formal adoption in October 2026.

### First Reading of Policies
The board approved the first reading of a list of policies, with a note that Policy 5200.01 had an explanatory memo attached regarding requested changes, to be directed to Dr. Lindell with any questions. No substantive debate occurred and the motion passed on a voice vote.

## Public Comment
No public comment was offered. The board noted at the opening of the public hearing and again at the start of the regular meeting that no members of the public had submitted comments.
