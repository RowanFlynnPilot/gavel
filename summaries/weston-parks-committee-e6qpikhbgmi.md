## Meeting Overview
The Village of Weston Parks Committee met on August 24, 2026, to hear a concept presentation from Everest Youth Hockey for a 30,000-square-foot flexible indoor community space at Kennedy Park, review a draft master plan for Mock Miller Park, and receive staff updates on park maintenance. The committee expressed general openness to further study of the hockey facility concept without committing any village funds, and reviewed the Miller Park master plan ahead of a planned public input meeting tentatively scheduled for September 3rd.

## Key Discussions
### Approval of Minutes – July 27, 2026
A committee member moved to accept the minutes as presented; a second was offered. The motion carried without opposition after a brief call for discussion.

### Public Comment – Kennedy Park Master Plan
Jim Pensel, 5002 Arrow Street, Weston, expressed disappointment that the Kennedy Park master plan contained no preliminary cost estimates, contrasting it with a prior master plan that included a $15 million figure. He also questioned the origins of proposed features like a band shell and food trucks, and raised concerns that key planning documents were being withheld from the committee packet.

### Everest Youth Hockey – Concept Proposal for Indoor Community Space at Kennedy Park
Brad Hansen (communications director), Nick Melsha (president), and Josh (vice president) of Everest Youth Hockey presented a concept for a 30,000-square-foot flexible indoor facility at the northwest corner of Kennedy Park, emphasizing year-round community use—farmers markets, childcare, recreation—with winter ice as the primary use. They stated they were not requesting funding, only endorsement to conduct a feasibility study largely funded by Everest itself. Committee members raised questions about overlap with Green Heck Turner Community Center, fundraising plans, and village financial exposure; Melsha noted that Green Heck's Aaron Mole is supportive and that their own prior $30,000 engineering study showed hockey-only sustainability. Committee members expressed general support for allowing further exploration, with one member noting 'I don't see the harm in letting them explore this further,' while another (Barb) cautioned that residents would not be happy if village money were committed at this stage. No formal vote was taken.

### Kennedy Park Baseball/Softball User Group Update
A representative identified as Olson, 5503 Wing Creek Drive, Weston, gave a brief update stating the group had productive discussions over the past month, expressed full support for youth hockey, and indicated willingness to alter plans and share space. The committee thanked the group for its social media outreach promoting shared park use and collaborative spirit with other user groups.

### Mock Miller Park Master Plan Review
Staff presented a concept master plan for Mock Miller Park incorporating existing features (T-ball fields, large field, shelter, restrooms) and proposed additions including a new playground, expanded walking trails, pickleball courts with shade, a food truck pavilion, outdoor fitness area, band shell with grass seating, and additional restrooms tied to a planned 2028 Ross Avenue utility reconstruction. Committee members discussed parking adequacy (estimated 120 stalls plus 30 additional proposed), potential for pickleball court expansion, the rationale for pickleball placement away from Kennedy Park due to noise concerns, and the importance of showing concept renderings of the band shell at the upcoming public input meeting. Staff clarified no cost estimates were requested yet pending committee and public input, and that a public input meeting is tentatively set for September 3rd at 6:00 p.m.

### Staff Report – Pool Season, Playground Inspections, Park Wall Repairs
Staff reported the pool season wrapped up August 16th, one week earlier than typical due to school starting. A final inspection of all seven new playgrounds was completed with Jessica Belowski; minor issues (loose bolts, cracked panel) were found and largely resolved, but three net structure pieces at Sand Hill Meadows need warranty replacement, possibly due to oversized users. Staff also noted a pool contractor will perform leak detection and pump valve replacement this fall, and that the concrete wall contractor (PGA) will return to address spalling at Kennedy Park retaining walls before a final walkthrough.

### Committee Remarks – Mountain Bay State Trail Expansion
A committee member raised the recurring interest of Trustee Hooshang Zeyghami in expanding the Mountain Bay State Trail beyond its current terminus. Staff clarified that Marathon County and the City of Wausau manage the trail under a state agreement, and that conversations have been ongoing but require resolution of whether expansion would serve pedestrian use or become a snowmobile corridor. Staff noted the segment from Messenger to Camp Phillips has had rails removed and that the village has requested a voice in planning discussions, but coordination with county and state parties remains ongoing.

### Committee Remarks – Sunflower Seed Policy at Turf Fields
A committee member raised the issue of sunflower seeds being consumed at the turf fields, noting signs in dugouts prohibit seeds on the turf but that enforcement and signage for spectator areas is lacking. The committee discussed establishing clear posted restrictions before next baseball season, with members acknowledging the problem involves both players and spectators. No formal action was taken but the committee agreed to revisit the policy before next season.
