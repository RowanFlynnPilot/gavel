Board Educational Meeting Pt.1

August 20, 2026
Hub City WindJoint Development AgreementRenewable Energy RegulationBody Art Convention LicensingHigher Education Partnership

The Marathon County Board of Supervisors held an educational meeting focused primarily on the Hub City Wind project and options available to the county, featuring extensive public comment overwhelmingly opposed to a Joint Development Agreement (JDA) with Alliant Energy, followed by legal presentations from attorneys Rebecca Roker and Bill Omin advising that a JDA is the county's best available tool given state preemption of local wind energy regulation. The meeting also included educational presentations on the NTC-UWSP partnership and an update on the Allison Dairland program. No formal votes were taken, as this was designated an educational meeting.

Discussion

Public Comment – Hub City Wind and JDA

Fifteen of the seventeen public speakers addressed the Hub City Wind project, with the overwhelming majority urging the board not to enter into a Joint Development Agreement with Alliant Energy. Speakers including Joe Bergen (Town of Day), Tren Spindler (Town of Day, chair of an intergovernmental committee of five affected townships), Cindy Nelson (Town of Opa), Jackie Dillinger (Town of Day), Nikki Catrell (Brighton Township), and others argued the county should instead pursue a limited moratorium and/or intervene at the PSC. Multiple speakers cited the county's own public survey and alternative energy committee recommendation against a JDA. Becky Tyro criticized the alternative energy task force as having produced no meaningful action despite overwhelming public opposition. One speaker, Harry Turkington from Madison, spoke in favor of a well-negotiated JDA, citing Marathon County's projected 5.5% population loss by 2050 and potential revenue benefits.

Public Comment – Cedar Creek Incfest Body Art Convention Licensing Fees (Resolution R5326)

Three speakers addressed proposed resolution R5326 regarding temporary body art event licensing fees. Brandy Candenberg (Village of Maine), organizing Cedar Creek Incfest coming to Rothschild in October, asked the board to vote no as presented, citing that the fee structure — roughly $208 per license covering six practitioners — could cost her event up to $14,000, compared to $130 in Sauk County and under $150 in Portage and Wood counties. Dave Brua, tourism marketing director for the Village of Rothschild, noted the event is expected to draw 6,000 attendees with a projected $500,000 economic impact. Mark Hannenburg, a local tattoo shop owner helping organize the event, argued the fee structure creates a 'cliff' where one additional practitioner doubles the cost and called for a more defensible fee methodology.

Standing Committee Report – County Highway XX Developer Agreement and Related Items

Supervisor Chris Dickinson (appearing remotely, initially experiencing audio issues) reported on three related items: ordinance amendment 032-26 for a four-way stop on County Highway XX, resolution R4826 urging the state DOT to increase truck length allowances on Highway 51, and an upcoming developer agreement. He explained a new facility planned on County Highway XX and Trellwood Lane requires trucks over 65 feet, which are technically restricted from Business 51 under state code — a restriction not currently enforced but not legally resolved. Because the state will not act until at least January 2027 due to elections, the developer sought a county agreement providing a legal route via County Highway XX to allow construction to begin this fall for a 2028 opening. The agreement would have the developer fund 75% of an intersection improvement and 50% of resurfacing for 2.3 miles of County Highway X, with a cancellation clause protecting the county from costs if the state resolves the issue sooner.

Educational Presentation: NTC and UWSP 'Purple and Blue Advantage' Partnership

NTC COO Darren Ackley and UWSP Dean of University College Gretle Stock presented plans to collocate UWSP at Wausau on NTC's Wausau campus beginning fall 2026, creating a seamless two-to-four-year degree pathway. Key points included that NTC associate degree graduates in the region earn an average of $54,000 annually, while a combined NTC-to-UWSP bachelor's degree pathway costs students approximately $30,000 compared to $75,000–$106,000 at a traditional four-year residential institution. They noted that only 28% of central Wisconsin residents hold bachelor's degrees versus 38% nationally, and that the partnership would place advisers from both institutions side by side to guide students. The board received the presentation and Chair Gibbs thanked the presenters; no questions from the board were noted on the record.

Hub City Wind – Legal Presentation on County Options (Item 9C7A)

Attorneys Rebecca Roker (Atollus Law, appearing virtually) and Bill Omin (UW-Madison Extension Law Center) presented to the board on the county's legal options regarding Hub City Wind. Both attorneys confirmed that under Wisconsin statutes (Chapter 196 and Chapter 66) and existing PSC case law, Marathon County is substantially preempted from regulating large-scale renewable energy projects. Omin stated he reviewed all relevant PSC dockets and found no instance where a local government's intervention caused the PSC to deny a utility's project application. Roker described moratoriums as 'frowned upon' under Wisconsin law and legally risky if the motivation is to halt development. Both attorneys advised that a JDA represents the best available mechanism for the county to address resident concerns, noting it is a contract that can include provisions on noise, road repairs, decommissioning, payments in lieu of taxes, and mitigation of unanticipated damages — though the developer is not obligated to agree to any specific term. During board Q&A, Supervisor Rosenberg asked whether a trust fund for remediation could be negotiated; attorneys said it was possible to ask for but must be supported by data and is subject to negotiation. Supervisor Wilhelm asked whether a JDA would release Alliant from unforeseen liability; Roker said a properly drafted JDA would include provisions holding Alliant responsible. Supervisor Aaron Griner pressed on the extent to which the PSC is legally obligated to consider county input, and Roker acknowledged there is 'no legal standard for consider' within the statute and that PSC has no obligation to consider a moratorium at all.

Action items

  • Board to continue deliberations on whether to authorize JDA negotiations with Alliant Energy / Hub City Wind
  • Board to consider resolution R5326 on body art temporary event licensing fees, with public commenters requesting the vote be delayed pending revision of the fee methodology
  • Supervisor Dickinson's developer agreement for County Highway XX to come before the board the following week
  • Board encouraged to consider updating the county comprehensive plan as one tool available in the PSC process
  • NTC and UWSP partnership colocation at NTC Wausau campus to begin fall 2026; county to continue working through transition of the Steuart Avenue UWSP campus building
