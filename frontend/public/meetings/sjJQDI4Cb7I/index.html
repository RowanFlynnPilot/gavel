City Council Meeting Pt.1

August 18, 2026
UtilitiesLead Service Line ReplacementPublic SafetyDevelopmentUWSP Campus Redevelopment

The Wausau Common Council met on August 18, 2026 and took action on several significant financial matters, including two lead service line replacement bond resolutions that passed 8-0 despite council frustration over being 'painted into a corner' by prior commitments. A resolution to hire a full-time HR Coordinator and a resolution approving a $50,000 feasibility study for a combined police/fire facility at the former UWSP Wausau campus both failed to achieve the required 2/3 majority, with the feasibility study vote potentially subject to reconsideration at the September 8th meeting. The council adjourned into closed session to deliberate on responses to RFPs for redevelopment of the former UWSP campus at 518 South 7th Avenue.

Discussion

File 26-0807: HR Coordinator hiring and budget modification

A motion was made by Larson and seconded by Kilian to approve the joint resolution from the Human Resources and Finance Committees to hire a full-time HR Coordinator with a related budget modification. No discussion was recorded in the transcript. The measure required a 2/3 majority (6 of 8 votes) and failed to achieve it, though the specific tally was not announced.

File 26-0806: UWSP/Police and Fire Facility Feasibility Study — $50,000 budget amendment

Alder Larson questioned where the $50,000 would come from (answer: reserves) and pressed for clarity on where future construction funds — potentially $20 million or more — would originate before spending on a feasibility study. Fire Chief Barnes explained that the study allocates $25,000 to FGMA Architects for the police side, $15,000 to Five Bugles for the fire side, and a reserve for potential environmental studies; she also outlined potential funding sources including $2 million allocated to the county from the UW system, congressionally directed spending, and state Department of Administration funds. Alder Kilian confirmed the plan would keep the fire department on the current footprint at Central Fire while expanding into the police department space, with 800 Grand Avenue potentially vacated. Alder Henke changed his position from committee (where he voted no) to support, citing newly learned funding opportunities. The motion by Trueblood, seconded by Tierney, failed to reach the required 2/3 majority; after the vote, Kilian raised the possibility of reconsideration at the September 8th meeting, and Chief Barnes asked whether returning with an amended funding structure that reduces the reserve draw could come back through committee.

File 26-0805: WEDC Brownfield Grant — 207 North 1st Avenue (former MBX property)

Motion by Henke, seconded by Neal. No discussion was recorded. The resolution passed 6-2.

File 26-0802: Mayoral appointments to commissions

Motion by Watson, seconded by Tierney to confirm the mayor's appointments to the police and fire commission, arts commission, and transit commission. No discussion. Passed 8-0.

File 26-0808: $3,469,102 Water System Revenue Bond Series 2026 — lead service line replacement (utility side)

Alder Kilian asked whether approval could lead to future water rate increases; Director Grote and Ehlers consultant John Cameron confirmed a rate increase is likely in the future — the current reserves are projected to run out by 2030 — but clarified this specific loan is approved by the state based on existing revenues, not a projected rate hike. Alder Trueblood raised concern that the council was being asked to approve bonds for work already underway and contracts already signed, calling it unorthodox and expressing frustration at 'building a plane while it's flying.' Alder Tierney questioned whether the program should be scaled back, as had been discussed in prior councils, and Director Grote confirmed those options would be presented at the next Finance Committee meeting. Alder Wiskowski expressed a desire to table the item for a full council but the mayor noted a quorum was present and business must proceed. Alder Henke said he would vote yes 'reluctantly,' citing the city's obligation to pay Community Infrastructure Partners for work already performed. The motion passed 8-0.

File 26-0809: Up to $3,331,289 General Obligation Promissory Note Series 2026B — lead service line replacement (homeowner side, includes $7.6M in principal forgiveness)

Motion by Watson, seconded by Trueblood. Alder Trueblood noted he was seconding 'with the clear understanding that we've been painted into a corner,' to which the mayor acknowledged 'torpedoes put in the water 4-5 years ago that are still coming.' No further substantive debate. Passed 8-0.

Suspension of Rules

A motion by Neal, seconded by Watson, to suspend rules — which had been listed to include both an athletic park item and the closed session — failed to achieve the required 2/3 majority. City attorney Fyfe then clarified that the closed session item had been properly agendized through committee and did not require suspension of rules, allowing the closed session motion to proceed separately.

Closed Session — RFPs for former UWSP Wausau Campus redevelopment

Alder Henke read the statutory basis (Wis. Stat. 19.85(1)(e)) — deliberating or negotiating for competitive or bargaining reasons — for a closed session to respond to requests for proposals for redevelopment of the former UWSP Wausau campus at 518 South 7th Avenue. Motion by Henke, seconded by Wiskowski. Roll call: Kilian-aye, Neal-aye, Wiskowski-aye, Henke-aye, Watson-abstain, Tierney-aye, Larson-no, Trueblood-aye. Passed 6-1 with one abstention.
