The Marathon County Board met to address the Hub City Wind project resolution from the Executive Committee, which lacked a concrete 'therefore be it resolved' clause. After extensive debate, the board voted to refer the matter back to the Executive Committee for a clearer recommendation, with supervisors expressing frustration over procedural issues and the county's limited authority to constrain the project given state PSC jurisdiction over large wind projects.

Discussion

Hub City Wind Project Resolution – Procedural Objection

Vice Chair Chris Dickinson raised an immediate procedural objection, arguing the resolution was invalid because it contained no 'therefore be it resolved' clause — effectively resolving nothing. Corp Counsel confirmed the resolution was intentionally written that way to allow the board to amend it in whatever direction they chose. Dickinson called this unprecedented in his experience and expressed concern about setting a bad procedural precedent.

Motion to Refer Back to Executive Committee

Dickinson moved to refer the matter back to the Executive Committee to develop a concrete recommendation and bring it to the September board meeting. Supervisor Caravelli supported the referral, noting the item had been confusing even at the Thursday discussion. Vice Chair Boots explained she had brought it to the full board so all 38 supervisors could weigh in, but expressed no objection to referring it back.

Robinson Amendment (Not Voted On)

Supervisor Robinson described a proposed amendment that would direct the County Administrator to send Alliant Energy the Alternative Energy Project Local Impact Advisory Committee report and request a contractual response addressing community concerns by October 1st. Corp Counsel ruled this amendment was not germane to the motion to refer and therefore out of order while the referral motion was on the floor. Robinson noted the 180-day PSC application window made timely action important.

Moratorium Debate and PSC Jurisdiction

Multiple supervisors including Gaul, Hagan, Hopper, and Sendelbach advocated for a moratorium, citing constituent demand and the project's unprecedented tower height of 620 feet. Corp Counsel and Rebecca (a PSC representative on the line) clarified repeatedly that while the county could legally pass a moratorium, the PSC is not required to consider it for projects over 100 MW, rendering it largely symbolic. Supervisor Gaul pushed for the board to investigate formal intervention in the PSC process as an alternative approach.

Vote to End Debate and Refer to Executive Committee

Supervisor Ritter called the previous question, which was seconded by Supervisor Krebs. The motion to end debate passed, though not unanimously. The board then voted on the referral motion, which also carried but was not unanimous. The matter will return to the Executive Committee for a recommendation before coming back to the full board.
