Discussion

Approval of creation of planning intern position

Community Development Director Carrie Edmonson presented the request for a temporary part-time planning intern to assist with public engagement for the mandatory comprehensive plan update, funded through an existing capital request with no additional budget impact. Alder Watson asked about funding sources and was told costs are covered through the comprehensive plan capital allocation. Alder Hincki raised concerns about whether an inexperienced intern could handle difficult public conversations; Edmonson clarified the intern would never conduct public engagement alone and would always work alongside experienced staff. Alder Neil asked about the ideal skill set, and Edmonson indicated UWSP students in land resources or planning would be the likely target pool. The item had previously passed unanimously at the HR Committee.

Approval of reclassification of HR Specialist to HR Generalist

Interim HR Director Ann Keenan presented the reclassification, noting the current specialist has been performing full generalist duties since the city moved to a two-person HR staff with Workday implementation, and faces a heavy workload including nine apprentice hires, police deputy recruitment, ADA compliance items, FMLA, and worker's comp. Alder Hincki expressed concern that the proposed pay range (up to the high $70s) exceeded what he found on Indeed and Glassdoor for the local market, though Keenan noted Marathon County's generalist pay aligns with the proposed range. Chair Martins argued it was inequitable to have someone doing generalist work capped at a specialist pay grade. Hincki voted no, with the motion passing over his dissent.

Establishing 2026 budget calendar dates

Finance Director Maryanne [last name not stated] presented four proposed budget workshop dates starting October 5, with sessions set at 6:30 p.m. to avoid conflicts with other committee meetings. She noted historical practice of canceling unneeded sessions. Alder Tierney emphasized she would not participate if budget books were not provided with ample advance notice, and abstained from the final vote. The calendar was approved with Tierney not voting.

Approval of Foundry on Third tax increment revenue bond

Economic Development Director Patrick [last name not stated] explained the municipal revenue obligation bond is required by the developer agreement with T-Wall as part of a pay-as-you-go TIF incentive structure, allowing the developer to show their lender a guarantee of future TIF payments. Finance staff clarified that the bond is backed by tax increment revenue from the district, not the general fund, and does not add to the city's general indebtedness. Alder Hincki asked about developer performance requirements and was told the developer is currently meeting minimum assessed value and construction cost thresholds in the agreement. The 2025 assessed value was $7.1 million and the 2026 value is $33.6 million. The motion was approved unanimously.

Discussion: options to pay for private-side lead service line replacement not covered by grants

City Engineer Eric [last name not stated] presented three options for covering the growing funding gap on private-side lead service line replacement as state and federal grant funds diminish. In 2026, the city covered approximately $3.3 million of the private-side funding gap. Alder Hincki expressed concern about equity between residents whose lines were replaced earlier at no cost versus those who may face charges later, and asked whether costs could be spread via special assessment over 10 years. Alder Watson asked about income-based or property-value-based subsidies rather than a flat dollar amount, and whether costs could be expressed as a percentage rather than a fixed sum. Alder Neil suggested accelerating replacements to take advantage of current lower costs and the expiring 0.25% loan rate in 2027. The discussion touched on potential use of TIF excess funds but noted council had previously removed lead service line language from multiple TID project plan amendments. No action was taken; the discussion will continue during the 2027 budget process.

Presentation from Ehlers regarding 2026A promissory note sale

Brian Rohr from Ehlers reported that the city's $10,560,000 general obligation promissory note sale in June received seven bids — described as extraordinary — with a low-to-high spread of only 17 basis points, indicating strong market demand. The notes closed July 9 at approximately 3.1% interest, saving about $52,000-$53,000 versus estimates. Moody's affirmed the city's Aa3 rating. The resulting debt adds about $382,000 to the 2027 debt levy, representing roughly a $6 change on a $350,000 property's tax bill. The city's general obligation debt stands at approximately 31% of its statutory 5% equalized value limit, with $164 million in remaining capacity. Alder Hincki asked about debt per capita comparisons with peer communities; Rohr agreed to provide that data. Finance Director Maryanne emphasized the city retires debt faster than most peers, using 10-year or shorter amortization schedules.

Presentation on equalized and assessed valuation and role within budget

Finance Director Maryanne delivered an educational presentation explaining Wisconsin's dual-value assessment system, levy limits tied to net new construction, expenditure restraint program incentives ($1.3 million grant received in 2026), and how TID closures affect levy capacity. She noted the city is now at 8.34% of its TID equalized value limit, well below the 12% cap, after recent district closures. Alder Hincki asked whether the city has policies against using long-term debt for operating expenses; Maryanne confirmed state rules effectively prevent this by requiring debt to match asset life. She also clarified that the firefighter referendum positions were successfully incorporated within the expenditure restraint program over three years, avoiding repayment obligations to the state.

Presentation on 2025 financial results

Rather than a full slide-by-slide walkthrough, Chair Martins and Alder Neil agreed the lengthy presentation would be deferred largely to questions, noting much of the content had been covered in earlier presentations. Maryanne indicated auditors are wrapping up the single audit and will present formally at a September regular meeting date to be determined. Alder Neil asked candidly whether the city's financial health is deteriorating, improving, or static; Maryanne indicated she had addressed comparative data in the presentation but the full answer was cut off in the transcript.
