The Wausau Public Health and Safety Committee met on August 17, 2026, handling routine license approvals, an ordinance update, and a contested rent abatement hearing for a tenant at 615 Elm Street who withheld approximately five months of rent due to persistent basement flooding, mold concerns, and maintenance failures. The committee approved all license matters and the nuisance abatement ordinance unanimously, then heard sworn testimony from the tenant, property management, and a city inspector before adjourning to closed session to deliberate the rent abatement request.

Discussion

Minutes of Preceding Meetings

Alder Tubbs moved to adopt the minutes as presented; Alder Watson seconded. The motion passed unanimously.

Approval or Denial of Various Licenses

The committee noted that Shelby Ingram and Darius Williams were absent for their license matters. Patrol Captain Pauls reported that Williams had previously provided insufficient paperwork; Pauls contacted probation and parole but could not obtain the records directly and had not heard back from Williams. The committee approved and denied licenses as written, including a pending recommendation for Camp 51, by unanimous vote.

Alcohol Beverage License Transfer - Vinaturo Wine Bar

Leslie Iverson appeared on behalf of Vinaturo Wine Bar, requesting a Class B beer and liquor license transfer from 209 Grand Street to 303 North 3rd Street at Anero Hotels LLC, citing limited seating, poor foot traffic, and ongoing construction at the current location. Alder Lukens moved to approve; Alder Watson seconded. The transfer was approved unanimously.

Repealing Chapter 9.28 Abatement of Nuisances

Discussion centered on the undefined role of 'health officer' in the ordinance. Alder Trueblood raised the question of whether the health officer referenced was a city or county official, noting that in emergencies city officers can be designated health officers but this ordinance pertains to a standing office typically held by the county. Alder Lukens suggested the director of inspections might serve that function in practice. Staff clarified the ordinance only changes the abatement process, not the underlying nuisance definitions. Alder Watson moved to approve the changes; Alder Lukens seconded. Passed unanimously, with the health officer designation flagged for further clarification.

Police, Fire, and Tavern Activity Reports

Patrol Captain Pauls highlighted that in June the new EMS chief successfully oversaw re-licensing of all paramedics with DHS, a significant three-year renewal process. Captain Pauls also noted that July's tavern report included demerit points assessed against licensed establishments where minors were served during tavern checks. No further discussion was held on the reports.

Rent Abatement Hearing - 615 Elm Street

Tenant Heather Fletcher testified she has lived at 615 Elm Street since June 2024, pays $990/month, and withheld approximately five months of rent (April through August) due to persistent basement flooding with standing water present since move-in, a leaking and improperly caulked shower, mold in the shower, improperly repaired windows (one clamped shut, constituting a fire hazard), and dehumidifiers running off her electricity plugged into a power strip sitting in standing water. She stated she first notified the landlord in January 2025 but repairs did not begin until August 4, 2026. Property manager Paul Dykstra and owner Mark Johnson of Better Tomorrow Investments testified they were not notified of water issues until May 4, 2026, when city violation notices arrived; they disputed the timeline and attributed basement water partly to the tenant's improperly installed air conditioning unit. City inspector Evan Garske testified he first inspected on April 29, 2026, confirmed standing water, insulation falling from basement venting, a hole above the shower head, and a window held up by a baseball bat. He found the shower and window repaired by a May 18 follow-up but standing water and insulation issues persisted until his August 13, 2026 inspection confirmed all violations corrected. He could not confirm mold and noted the department is not trained to enforce mold issues, referring those to Marathon County Health. The committee then adjourned to closed session to deliberate.
