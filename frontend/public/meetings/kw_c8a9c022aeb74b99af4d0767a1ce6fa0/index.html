Approval of Minutes - June 15th Meeting

Trustee Mary Solheim moved to approve the June 15th meeting minutes as presented, seconded by Misty Swanson. The motion passed 4-0 with no discussion.

Property Reassessment Update

Finance Director John reported that the exterior reassessment will begin the week of August 24th, with five assessors working in the field. He explained the village is approximately 40% below required full market value and faces a state takeover of the reassessment — at two to three times the cost — if not completed by end of 2027. The reassessment is being funded through budget surpluses from prior years when positions were unfilled, avoiding any new budget line item. Trustee Guy Fridale raised concerns about negative social media commentary and suggested early, repeated public messaging that the village only controls its portion of the tax levy. John noted that no valuation changes will appear on the December 2026 tax bill and that some property owners seeing a below-average valuation increase could actually see a tax reduction. The committee also discussed reviewing potentially tax-exempt parcels that may no longer qualify.

2026 Preliminary Equalized Valuation and TID Increments

John reported that the village's equalized valuation rose by approximately $101 million in 2026, nearly a 10% increase, now exceeding $1.2 billion, but that assessed valuation remains around $700 million — only about 63% of full market value. Net new construction came in at just 0.65% for the third consecutive year, limiting the village's allowable tax levy increase without an exception to approximately $12,000. TID 1 was identified as the village's most significant financial concern: a 12% drop in valuation from $21 million to $19 million resulted from assessor adjustments on two parcels near Kowalski and Highway 39, compounded by a state-issued prior year correction of $1.4 million. John emphasized that if the anticipated distribution center in TID 1 does not break ground this fall, the village could face a shortfall of $1.5 million or more by 2044, potentially depleting the general fund reserve built over recent years. Fridale observed that TIDs 2 and 4 are performing well and noted TID 3 closed in February.

July 2026 Budget Status Report

John reported that after seven months (58% of the year), the general fund had collected approximately 62% of budgeted revenues and spent about 49% of budgeted expenditures, indicating a healthy position. He cautioned that public works billing typically lags field work and that costs will increase. Three ongoing budget concerns — village attorney fees, municipal building costs, and garbage — remain unresolved. John acknowledged the 2025 audit has not yet been completed due to competing priorities including the reassessment launch and 2027 budget preparation, but expressed confidence that the audit could be finalized in time for the October budget workshops. Committee members praised John's work stabilizing village finances and noted the village operates with limited finance staff.

Review of Existing Debt Obligations Outstanding

John reviewed all outstanding debt, totaling approximately $14.5 million, of which $10 million is general obligation debt — representing only 17% of the village's $59 million maximum borrowing capacity. He identified the 2016 TID 1 redevelopment lease revenue bonds as a wait-and-see situation given low interest rates of 2.45-3.2% and the pending distribution center development. The 2024 general obligation bonds at 4-5% interest were flagged as a future refinancing candidate once callable in April 2029. He highlighted that debt service tax levy obligations drop significantly in 2029 (by ~$226,000) and 2030 (by ~$165,000), creating an opportunity window for infrastructure investment. Fridale and others emphasized the importance of TID 2 and TID 4 closure timelines — estimated around 2032 and 2033 respectively — as additional fiscal opportunities.

2027-2036 Capital Improvement Program Budget

John and Public Works Director Greg presented the village's first-ever 10-year Capital Improvement Program, drafted using dedicated CIP software rather than Excel to improve accuracy and fluidity. The draft CIP covers projects across fire, municipal center, parks, police, public works, sewer, and water utilities, with a preliminary 2027 total of approximately $4.3 million. Funding sources include approximately $1.2 million in unused TID 2 bond proceeds that must be redirected if not spent on a TID project by October 2027, potential borrowing, utility depreciation funds, and donations. John noted the parks committee provided additional input the prior evening and that figures will be updated before the September presentation. The committee discussed potential borrowing scenarios — ranging from $1 million to $10 million — and agreed that a formal affordability analysis presenting multiple options would be prepared for the September APC meeting and budget workshops. No final decisions were made.
