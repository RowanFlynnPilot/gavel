Marathon County · County Board

Board Educational Meeting Pt.2

August 20, 2026
ZoningBudgetBattery Energy Storage SystemsHighway FacilityData Centers

The Marathon County Board held a lengthy educational meeting reviewing upcoming voting items including zoning ordinance amendments for battery energy storage systems, a data center moratorium, and a large electric generating facility ordinance. The board also received an extensive briefing on plans for a new county highway facility estimated at $50-55 million, with staff presenting a financing strategy using existing debt and North Central Healthcare revenues to avoid new long-term borrowing. No binding votes were taken as this was an educational/review session ahead of the formal voting meeting.

Discussion

Appointment of Aaron Griner as Infrastructure Committee Chair

Supervisor Arstead questioned why the committee's vice chair was passed over for the chair position, noting that prior practice had been for the vice chair to assume the role when a chair vacates. Chair Gibbs explained that he and Vice Chair Dickinson reviewed supervisors by tenure and recommended Griner. Supervisor Robinson asked whether a new vice chair would be designated; Gibbs confirmed the existing vice chair remains in place.

Battery Energy Storage Systems Ordinance (0-27-26)

Zoning Administrator Shad Harvey explained the ordinance updates an existing BEST chapter to add emergency response plan requirements, first responder training and PPE obligations, system owner liability for EMS costs, and clearer decommissioning standards. Supervisor Sundowski announced he planned to offer two amendments at the voting meeting: an 1,800-foot setback from dwellings and waterways, and a secondary concrete containment requirement. Harvey cautioned that an 1,800-foot setback would effectively prohibit BEST systems in Marathon County given its wetlands, and questioned whether rigid secondary containment could interfere with manufacturer safety requirements. Supervisor Gao raised concerns about distinguishing residential from commercial BEST systems and appropriate setbacks for larger systems above 5 megawatts. Supervisor Ritter argued the ordinance should be passed now and amended later as more information becomes available; Gao agreed. The board reached consensus to move forward.

Data Center Moratorium Ordinance (0-28-26)

Harvey explained the proposed 12-month moratorium would allow the county to develop a zoning ordinance for data centers, as none currently exists. Supervisor Gao cautioned that public perception of the moratorium as an outright ban on data centers is inaccurate, stating the moratorium only provides time to draft regulations. Supervisor Leur clarified the moratorium applies only to county-zoned towns, not incorporated municipalities or unzoned townships.

Large Electric Generating Facility Ordinance (0-29-26)

Harvey explained that because Wisconsin Statute 196.401 grants the PSC authority over facilities over 100 megawatts once a CPCN is issued, the county cannot regulate such facilities directly. The ordinance is designed to strengthen county notification within the PSC process so the board can decide whether to intervene. Supervisor Gao strongly supported the ordinance, noting the Town of Easton situation as an example where a solar farm proceeded without the county board having any advance information.

Parks Department Fee Ordinance (0-30-26)

County Administrator Leonard requested that the ordinance be pulled from the voting agenda, explaining that a broader review of all county ordinances containing fee references is needed to eliminate conflicts between ordinances and the fee schedule. Leonard's goal is to remove specific dollar amounts from ordinances and replace them with references to the fee schedule, with a target of completing that review within a month. The board indicated a committee member would formally move to pull Ordinance 0-30-26 at the voting meeting.

Highway Facility Phase 3 Resolution (Resolution 49-26)

Staff presented a two-part resolution: directing finalization of design and release of bid documents to determine actual cost of the new highway facility (previously estimated at $50-55 million), and directing staff to build the 2027 budget around a financing strategy that shifts North Central Healthcare debt service revenues to a project fund rather than issuing new long-term bonds. Financial advisor Kristen Hansen reported current municipal bond rates at approximately 4.16-4.17% for 20-year general obligation notes, compared to the county's existing debt issued at roughly 1.2-3.8%. Administrator Leonard explained the strategy avoids new 20-year borrowing by transitioning existing debt to debt levy over roughly two years and accumulating North Central Healthcare revenues over six to seven years. The highway fund balance totals approximately $79.4 million, of which $30 million is reserved for the highway shop, leaving only $91,000 truly unallocated. Supervisors questioned phased construction, staging of the West Street facility divestment, and contingency plans if North Central Healthcare revenues fall short. The City of Wausau has expressed interest in the West Street property.

Tattoo and Piercing Fee Schedule (Resolution 53-26)

Administrator Leonard and Health Department representative Kate Floric explained that the county's interpretation of DSPS regulations requires licensing each individual practitioner area separately at temporary events, which organizers of the Cedar Creek Inkfest convention argued would be costly. Staff proposed a tiered fee structure allowing up to six practitioners to share one license. Supervisor Pool proposed amending the resolution to delay the effective date until after the mid-October Cedar Creek Inkfest event to avoid disrupting the organizers on short notice. Supervisor Mash expressed support for Pool's proposal, and Administrator Leonard agreed staff would meet with event organizers to clarify how many shops would be involved and how the existing versus proposed fee structure would apply to them.
