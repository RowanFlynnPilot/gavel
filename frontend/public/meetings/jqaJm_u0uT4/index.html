City Council Meeting Pt.2

August 18, 2026

The Wausau City Council reconvened from closed session to consider two major items: approving submission of an RFP response to Marathon County for the former UWSP Wausau campus at 518 South 17th Avenue, and approving an alternate bid for construction of up to 153 parking stalls in Riverfront Parking Lot E. Both items passed 6-2 and 6-1 (with one abstention) respectively, with the campus proposal representing a potential major relocation of the Wausau Police Department and other city functions, while the parking lot project fulfills obligations under a developers agreement related to Athletic Park.

Discussion

RFP Response for Former UWSP Wausau Campus

City Director Randy Fyfe outlined the proposal, which includes relocating Wausau PD headquarters to the main campus building (potentially partnering with the Wausau School District), exploring public safety or recreation use for the field house, demolishing dormitories for new housing, converting the current police department building to a fire station, and identifying 815 Floral Avenue as a redevelopment opportunity. The purchase price offered is $1, with a requested 6-month due diligence period. Alder Larson expressed skepticism, citing past city land purchases that yielded no results and questioning the cost of maintaining a 66-year-old building versus DPW facility needs. Alder Watson, who serves on both the city council and county board, recused herself from active debate but voiced support. Alder Kilian raised concerns about including the Floral Avenue property in the RFP, and Fyfe indicated it could be removed at council's discretion. Alderwoman Trueblood emphasized the vote only commits the city to submitting the proposal, not to any final decision, and there is no cost to submit.

Procedural Dispute: Calling the Question on UWSP Campus RFP

Alderwoman Trueblood moved to call the question on the UWSP campus RFP vote; an unnamed alderperson objected, triggering a procedural debate over council rules on terminating debate. After consultation with council rules, the objection was ultimately withdrawn, allowing the vote to proceed without a separate vote to call the question. A separate motion to suspend the rules related to item 6 (reconsideration of referral of resolution 26-0811) passed 6-2, with the presiding officer confirming that 6-2 constitutes the required two-thirds majority of members present.

Resolution 26-0811: Riverfront Parking Lot E Alternate Bid (PGA Inc.)

Director Lindman explained that the city is obligated under a developers agreement to provide approximately 150 new parking spaces near Athletic Park by May 2027. A city attorney opinion concluded the legal obligation was closer to 118 stalls given prior lot construction, but the developer Mark McDonald anticipated 153. The Board of Public Works awarded the base bid to PGA Inc. and referred the alternate bid (adding stalls up to 153) to the council. The base bid came in at $500,000 and the alternate bid added $57,500, totaling under the $650,000 budget. Alder Henning called it a 'no-brainer' given the savings. Alder Witkowski raised concerns about year-round utilization of the large lot, garbage issues near Athletic Park, and parking enforcement during ball games. Alder Tierney stated she would not support the alternate bid above 120 stalls, calling the extra stalls a want rather than a need. The motion passed 6-2.
