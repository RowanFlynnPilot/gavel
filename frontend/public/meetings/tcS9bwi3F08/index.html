Discussion

Nonprofit Funding - Women's Community ($55,000)

Jane Graham Jennings, executive director of the Women's Community, presented on how the organization uses its $55,000 annual county contract, noting the funding covers approximately 16% of the $348,822 cost of contracted services and is used to leverage an additional $350,000 in state and federal grants. She highlighted services including lethality assessment protocol screening with the sheriff's department, domestic violence advocacy, sexual assault education in schools, and child advocacy center support. County Administrator Leonard indicated his plan is to keep nonprofit funding flat in the 2027 budget.

Sheriff's Office Fleet Strategy and Rolling Stock

Finance Director Chris (last name not clearly stated) presented a memo outlining a plan developed with Enterprise fleet services to address the gap in vehicle acquisitions caused by flat funding and failure to follow through on a CPI-based increase plan. The plan involves rolling existing leases, extending others with remaining mileage, and moving 17 vehicles into the lease fleet program, with a projected funding gap of $138,876 between existing rolling stock funds and the proposed $456,548 CIP allocation for 2027. Committee members discussed the bottleneck caused by upfitting vehicles at Belco in Columbus, and the sheriff described an effort to pursue a state innovation grant to create a regional upfitting operation involving Wausau, Mountain Bay, and Kronenwetter police departments. Administrator Leonard confirmed outreach to NTC, McDevco, and WATTA workforce organizations to support that effort. No resolution was presented, as fleet funding is not formally approved until November budget adoption.

Jail Stay Fees

Sheriff's Captain Chad (last name not clearly stated) presented a memo explaining that while the jail collected $303,666 in total fees in 2025, the portion attributable specifically to first-day fees ($30) and daily pay-for-stay fees ($20) is much smaller because roughly 90% of the jail population is pre-sentenced and not assessed those charges. Staff noted the population is largely indigent, making collections very difficult, and that even fee increases would not meaningfully increase actual revenue. Administrator Leonard noted a housekeeping amendment will be brought forward to update ordinance section 5.11 to reference the fee schedule rather than listing specific amounts, after an oversight was identified where the old fee remained in the ordinance text.

New Position Requests and Reclassifications for 2027 Budget

Administrator Leonard presented four personnel actions: (1) DA's office - abolish a county-funded restitution specialist position and create a 1.0 victim witness specialist and 0.5 administrative specialist, leveraging state Chapter 950 reimbursement; (2) Sheriff's office - abolish a senior admin position and create a corrections officer position to address jail staffing needs; (3) Clerk of Courts - reclass an administrative assistant to administrative specialist to manage scheduling duties previously defunded; (4) Medical Examiner - abolish the vacant deputy chief medical examiner (physician) position and create a senior administrative assistant, generating significant cost savings. Medical Examiner Director Jess explained the physician position was unsustainable due to lower-than-projected case volumes, noting the office is building volume gradually with new county and hospital contracts. The committee approved forwarding all positions to HR, Finance and Property.

Marathon County Ordinance 5.09 - Chief Deputy Appointment Update

Sheriff Chad presented an ordinance update required by 2025 Wisconsin Act 196, which merged the chief deputy and under sheriff roles so that the chief deputy automatically assumes the sheriff's duties if needed, and removed the chief deputy position from civil service oversight, making it solely the sheriff's appointment. Corporation Counsel Brian Desmond also recommended removing a provision barring the chief deputy from seeking the office of sheriff, citing both practical concerns about limiting the candidate pool and potential legal issues with restricting access to elective office. The committee approved forwarding the ordinance amendment to the county board.

Non-Lapsing Contingency Account for Medical Examiner's Office

Administrator Leonard proposed creating a non-lapsing contingency account capped at $250,000 for the Medical Examiner's office, funded by year-end operating surpluses rather than levy, to cover volatile revenue years and expensive capital equipment needs such as the $300,000 full-body X-ray (Phobus) machine. Supervisor John Robinson expressed support but raised concerns that the $250,000 cap is high compared to other departments' accounts and that frequent periodic reviews should ensure the funds are not used as a budget workaround. Supervisor Jason raised questions about whether building expenses like roofs would draw from this fund, and Leonard confirmed it could serve as a blended funding source with board approval via supermajority vote. The committee approved forwarding the proposal to the county board.

6A - Flock Safety ALPR Technology Presentation

Captain Jeff Stefanic of the sheriff's investigations division began an educational presentation on automated license plate reader (ALPR) technology, explaining the sheriff's office has used Flock Safety systems since 2023. He described how stationary cameras capture and read license plates using infrared illumination and character-recognition software, noting the technology is used commercially in toll systems and car washes. The presentation was ongoing at the end of the transcript and covered what the system is, its capabilities, data retention policies, and public safety outcomes, with the stated goal of educating the committee amid public questions generated by recent news coverage.
