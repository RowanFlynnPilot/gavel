Discussion

Welcome of New Commissioner Lisa Nowak and Appointment of Secretary

The commission president welcomed Lisa Nowak as the newest member, filling the seat vacated by Cheryl Barr, with a term ending April 30, 2028. Her HR background was cited as an asset. The president also announced the appointment of V Lesky as commission secretary, citing his legal background, to fill the vacancy left by Cheryl Barr's retirement from the commission.

Approval of Minutes (July 27, June 22, August 3)

The commission president noted that the June 22nd minutes were returning for correction — a date error listed 'April 2026' had been corrected to 'April 2025' in reference to when a complaint started. A motion was made to approve all three sets of minutes together; it was seconded and passed unanimously with no objection.

Discussion and Possible Action on Deputy Chief Promotional Process

The commission president outlined a new, more robust internal deputy chief promotional process developed collaboratively with the police chief, framed as an early step in chief succession planning given that the deputy chief is retiring in January 2027 and the chief intends to retire in early 2028. The process would include listening sessions with officers, detectives, clerical staff, and lieutenants; formal candidate presentations to the commission on financial and operational challenges; written department assessments; panel interviews; and the chief's one-on-one interviews before making a final recommendation. Commissioners expressed general support, with one asking whether public participation should be incorporated; the president suggested that level of public involvement may be more appropriate at the chief selection stage. The chief suggested that annual listening sessions with staff could benefit the commission's ongoing understanding of department culture. The commission agreed to proceed, targeting applications in September, screening in November, and a decision at the December PFC meeting. A commissioner flagged that a special December meeting may be needed given potential overlap with a police hiring process.

Fire Department Monthly Update (Chief Cobb)

Fire Chief Cobb reported that the apprenticeship program is on track with 45 applicants for nine positions, led by Deputy Chief Ploussard in partnership with NTC. However, the department remains six positions down with another retirement in December. One of two candidates from a prior special meeting failed the psychological exam and will not advance; the other is awaiting a physical agility test delayed by a broken stair stepper at NTC. The chief also noted significant budget pressure, with direction from the mayor's office to cut approximately 2% from the budget, requiring creative solutions alongside Deputy Chief.

Police Department Monthly Update (Chief Barnes)

Chief Barnes described a difficult recent period including a use-of-force incident that went viral on social media, generating hundreds of critical emails, as well as a fatal fire and multiple death-related calls affecting officer mental health. The department has three current openings and anticipates six retirements in early 2027, creating a need to hire nine officers — historically difficult in a single process. The chief said 22 candidates are currently in the queue with the application period still open. He explained the complexity of the state's maintenance-of-effort requirement tied to shared revenue, noting that the penalty for falling short far exceeds the ~$300,000 in budget cuts being requested. He expressed optimism about 2027 and mentioned a fundraiser for 24-year-old Officer Rafael 'Buddy' Carey, who is battling stage four cancer. The commission president suggested adding the annual report to the next agenda for further questions.
