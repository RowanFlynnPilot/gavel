The Marathon County Environmental Resources Committee held its August 4, 2026 meeting and took action on several zoning matters, including approving recommendations on two rezoning petitions, advancing an updated battery energy storage systems ordinance (with an amendment increasing residential setback to 300 feet), approving a 12-month moratorium on data centers in county-zoned towns, and beginning discussion of a new large electric generating facilities ordinance. A notable announcement opened the meeting: the City of Marshfield voluntarily withdrew a rezoning request related to solar because the county's solar ordinance is more restrictive than state law, which prohibits counties from requiring a rezone for solar projects.

Discussion

Marshfield Solar Rezoning Withdrawal Announcement

Chair Ritter announced before public comment that the City of Marshfield voluntarily withdrew its request to rezone from rural estate to general agriculture. The withdrawal stems from Marathon County's solar ordinance being more restrictive than state law, which does not permit counties to require a rezone for solar projects. Corporation counsel's determination will be added to the packet for committee review, and the item was removed from the agenda.

Public Comment

Six speakers addressed the committee during general public comment, with the majority supporting a moratorium on hyperscale data centers and expressing support for commercial solar and wind energy development. Speakers cited climate change, water resource protection, grid resilience, and public health concerns. One speaker, Jay Caldwell, argued that 'people don't need data centers — billionaires need data centers to control us,' while others like Dan Barth and Jean Abreu framed renewable energy support in economic and environmental terms. Nikki Cottrell, speaking online, urged the committee to place industrial-scale wind development on a future agenda and adopt a moratorium pending independent environmental review.

5A1: J Good Rezoning - Farmland Preservation to Rural Estate and General Agriculture, Town of Brighton

Staff member Garrett Pagel presented the staff report recommending approval of the rezone of approximately 37.68 acres — 5 acres to rural estate and 32.68 acres to general agriculture — at the intersection of State Highway 153 and County Road F. The Town of Brighton recommended approval on June 12, 2026. Supervisor Griesbach asked about churches as permitted uses in rural estate zoning and was told churches are a permitted use in that district. No public testimony was offered for, against, or as interested. The committee voted to approve and recommend to the County Board.

5A2: Weinkauf Rezoning - General Agriculture to Rural Residential, Town of Gunther

Staff presented the rezone request for a parcel at the corner of Duberstein Road and Whiz Road. Supervisor Drewek raised a concern that the Town of Gunther's resolution did not have any written answers to the required questions on page 30, noting it could set a bad precedent. Staff confirmed the town had approved the rezone but left the resolution incomplete. The committee agreed to send the application back to the town to properly complete the resolution before placing it on a future ERC agenda. The public hearing was completed with no public testimony before the motion was made.

5A4: Battery Energy Storage Systems Ordinance Amendment

Staff Chad Harvey presented updates to the existing battery energy storage ordinance, including clearer hazard mitigation and emergency response plan requirements, a minimum 7-foot fence height, first responder training obligations for commercial systems, 24/7 monitoring requirements, and refined decommissioning provisions. Public testimony from Town of Easton Chairman Dean Beck raised concerns about volunteer fire department capacity, water availability for firefighting lithium battery fires, and groundwater contamination risk. Another speaker questioned whether setbacks of 100-200 feet were adequate given fire risks. Supervisor Drewek moved and Supervisor Kurth seconded an amendment to increase the residential setback for commercial battery systems from 200 feet to 300 feet. The amendment passed with at least one 'no' vote (not unanimous). The full amended ordinance was then moved to the County Board by Supervisor Lemmer, seconded by Supervisor Kurth, and approved.

5A5: Data Center Moratorium Ordinance

Staff presented a proposed 12-month moratorium on new data center development in county-zoned towns to allow time to research and develop a comprehensive data center ordinance. Chair Ritter and Chair Gibbs clarified the moratorium applies only to the 18 county-zoned towns, not cities, villages, or other towns. Staff proposed an amendment to close a potential loophole by applying the 5,000-square-foot accessory use exemption on a cumulative basis per parcel. The City of Marshfield rezone withdrawal was noted as context. Supervisor Coggs noted 64 data centers already exist in Wisconsin, with large ones in Pleasant Prairie, Port Washington, and a project near Beaver Dam/Dodgeville, and urged careful size-differentiated research during the moratorium. The committee approved the staff-proposed amendment and then approved the moratorium as amended.

5A6: Large Electric Generating Facilities Ordinance

Staff introduced a new ordinance — described as among the first of its kind in Wisconsin — requiring large electric generating facility developers to submit to the county the same engineering plans submitted to the Public Service Commission, within 30 days of filing with the PSC, for projects in county-zoned towns. Corporation counsel clarified the ordinance cannot compel developers to comply but creates a framework to open dialogue and inform county decision-making about whether to intervene in PSC proceedings or negotiate agreements. Supervisor Drewek asked why the review window was 30 rather than 60 days; Supervisor Lemmer suggested 45 days. Staff acknowledged the 30-day timeline is challenging and that outside expertise may be needed to review engineering documents. No votes on this item were captured in the transcript.
