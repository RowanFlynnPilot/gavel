Discussion

Public Comment

Six residents spoke during public comment. Jim Pensol encouraged civic engagement and described how public turnout once reversed a board decision to rename Schofield Avenue to DC Everest Boulevard. Kyra Lenzo addressed the animal fancier licensing issue, defending her care of four huskies and offering to expand her fenced outdoor space to comply with any space requirements. A neighbor, identified as a Borcart family member at 604 North Lane, complained about waste runoff from Lenzo's dogs onto their property citing ordinance sections 10.12, 10.19, and 10.29. Scott Borcart (County Road H) and Tony Hase (Danny's Girls vendor) both urged the board to keep the farmers market at Kennedy Park, with Hase noting business had improved significantly since the Alderson roundabout opened. Steve Mel spoke on the rezoning item (agenda item 43), arguing the planning commission's denial was improperly influenced by planner Jennifer Higgins's personal preference against storage facilities and citing Wisconsin Act 67's limitations on CUP denials.

Joint Resolution 2026-0001: Mountain Bay Metro PD Headquarters Debt Authorization

Trustee Ermeling raised significant concerns about the repayment structure, noting that at $130,000 per year minimum, it could take approximately 34-40 years for Mountain Bay Metro PD to fully repay the municipalities for the construction loan, while Weston would need to increase its levy by roughly $206,000 annually to service the 20-year debt. Village Administrator and police representatives clarified that $130,000 is a minimum and that the Oversight Board can direct additional fund balance payments to accelerate repayment, with a goal of $250,000-$275,000 annually total from both municipalities. Ermeling and another trustee (believed to be Kern) voted no; the resolution passed 3-2. The agreement had been reviewed by Rothschild's financial advisors and Mountain Bay Metro's attorney but not Weston's own attorney, which Ermeling flagged as a concern for a multi-million-dollar commitment.

Farmers Market Location at Kennedy Park

The board voted 4-1 to continue the farmers market at Kennedy Park, with Trustee Ermeling casting the lone dissenting vote, expressing concern that the park's green space was being consumed. Trustee Olson outlined several proposed mitigation measures including enhanced parking enforcement, coning off intersections 15 feet in each direction, and reaching out to a neighboring property owner about sight-obscuring vegetation. Trustee Lopes-Serrao argued the market pays for itself, serves as a community gathering event, and that no viable alternative location exists. Village President Maloney said he has visited the market at least ten times and found it a positive experience. A suggestion to survey vendors and customers again after the Alderson roundabout opened was raised by Ermeling and supported by others as a future step.

Municipal Center Rooms as Public Meeting/Gathering Spaces

The board discussed but took no action on a proposal to open municipal center meeting rooms to the public for gatherings, a topic raised at a prior community engagement session. Multiple trustees and staff expressed opposition based on past experience with damage, cleaning costs, and supervision burdens — Clerk Pam described specific problems from her time at Schofield Township, including vandalism and post-party cleanup that exceeded security deposits. Trustee Olson said he was not firmly opposed but would not support it without an alternative to requiring staff supervision on weekends. No action was taken.

Rezoning Request: 4205 & 4307 Weston Avenue (B2 to LI) — Storage Facility

Trustee Lopes-Serrao moved to accept the Planning Commission's recommendation of denial for the rezoning of 4205 and 4307 Weston Avenue from B2 Highway Business to LI Limited Industrial for a proposed personal storage unit facility. A procedural issue was raised: overturning the planning commission's denial requires a 2/3 supermajority (five of seven votes), but with only five trustees present, all five would need to vote in favor of rezoning — effectively requiring unanimity to override. The motion to accept the denial received a second, and the transcript ends before the vote is recorded.

2027 Budget Priorities

The Finance and HR Committee brought three preliminary priorities to the full board: one robotic mower pilot, a new facilities and grounds maintenance position, and three SAFER grant-funded fire/EMS staff positions phased in over 2027. Trustees discussed broadening the scope to include public safety (EMS, fire, police), drinking water infrastructure, code enforcement, and cybersecurity. Trustee Beck emphasized multi-year budget planning similar to the CIP process used in public works. A recent village comprehensive plan survey was cited showing road maintenance and EMS tied as top community priorities. No formal action was taken; further discussion is scheduled for a budget session on the 24th at 5:30.

Ordinance 26-013: Nuisance Abatement Property Storage

The ordinance establishing a 30-day storage and disposal procedure for property removed during nuisance abatement was approved unanimously with brief discussion. Trustees agreed the 30-day window was reasonable to give property owners time to retrieve belongings.

Ordinance 26-014: Animal Fancier Ordinance Amendment

The amendment to Section 10.123, which shifts animal fancier hearings from the Planning Commission to the Eclipse body, was approved unanimously after brief clarification that this item was about the procedural change, not the specific Lenzo dog license case addressed in public comment.
