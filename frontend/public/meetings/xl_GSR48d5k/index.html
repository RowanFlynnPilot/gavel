The Marathon County Executive Committee held a lengthy meeting focused on the Hub City Wind project in southwestern Marathon County, featuring public comment from 10 residents, a panel discussion with legal experts and municipal officials, and concluding with a motion to forward the question of whether to pursue a Joint Development Agreement (JDA) to the full 38-member county board. The committee heard from attorneys, a UW-Extension local government educator, and a town chair from Wood County about the limited legal authority counties have over large-scale wind projects under Wisconsin statute, with experts uniformly concluding that a JDA is effectively the strongest tool available to the county.

Discussion

Public Comment – Hub City Wind Project

Ten speakers addressed the committee on the Hub City Wind project. Speakers were divided: several opposed the project citing concerns about tourism impacts on Rib Mountain viewsheds, farmland destruction, health effects, wildlife harm, road damage, and skepticism about JDA benefits, with Joe Bergen urging the county to control any JDA drafting and Nikki Catrell calling for a moratorium and intervenor status. Supporters Dan Bar and Kathy Conley Bar argued for renewable energy as a climate response. Wendy Rogolski specifically demanded that any decision to pursue JDA negotiations be made in open session with a recorded vote, not behind closed doors. James Yeetus urged the county to fight the project through litigation or ordinance rather than negotiate.

Approval of July 15, 2026 Executive Committee Meeting Minutes

Chair Gibbs called for a motion on the minutes. Supervisor Robinson moved and Supervisor Morash seconded. No discussion was offered and the minutes were approved unanimously.

Staff Presentation: PSC Intervention Process and County Research Memos

CPZ Director Lori Kimmons presented two memos. The first summarized conversations with PSC staff about the intervention process, noting counties have a 14-day window to apply for intervenor status after a notice of proceeding, that the PSC staff could not recall any project ever being fully denied through intervention, and that intervention is costly and time-consuming. The second memo summarized conversations with staff and officials in Columbia, Iowa, Kenosha, St. Croix counties and the Town of Saratoga in Wood County, all of whom reported using JDAs as their primary tool and recommended early negotiation over PSC intervention. Staff's overall recommendation was to begin engaging the developer as soon as possible about a potential JDA.

Panel: Town of Saratoga Experience with JDA

Laura Fure, Town Chair and Zoning Administrator for the Town of Saratoga in Wood County, described her community's experience with a 150-megawatt solar project owned outright by the developer (not leased). The town hired an independent Madison law firm paid for by the developer to draft the JDA, required a decommissioning plan, and receives approximately $350,000 per year in shared revenues (county receives about two-thirds). She said the biggest surprises were the size and danger of battery storage systems, enlarged transmission line easements, and that these unknowns are now being addressed in a second solar JDA currently underway. She stated the town felt it had significant control through the JDA process.

Panel: Legal Authority and JDA Framework – Andy Phillips (Atollis Law) and Bill Oman (UW Extension)

Attorney Andy Phillips explained that under Wisconsin statute 66.0401(1m), counties can regulate renewable energy projects on a case-by-case basis but that statute 196.491 preempts local regulation for projects 100 megawatts or more once the PSC issues a CPCN, meaning the county has no authority to stop such a project through ordinance. He said JDAs are the most effective tool counties have to address decommissioning, road damage, setbacks, and emergency response. Bill Oman of UW-Extension agreed entirely, noting the legislature deliberately preempted local authority to promote renewable energy, that legislative efforts to restore local control failed in the most recent session, and that updating a comprehensive plan is the only other tool available but that the PSC has never denied a project based on inconsistency with a local comp plan. Both strongly recommended JDAs as the primary protective mechanism.

Executive Committee Q&A with Panelists

Supervisors asked wide-ranging questions. On moratoriums, Phillips said a short-duration moratorium (6–18 months) aimed at developing new regulations is legally defensible but one designed simply to block a project would likely be struck down; Oman agreed. On intervention, Phillips noted his firm has filed appeals in two cases where JDA negotiations broke down after a CPCN was issued. On road damage, Phillips recommended addressing repairs proactively in a JDA rather than relying on retrospective treble-damage statutes (86.02 and 86.14). On decommissioning, Phillips said JDAs should require financial assurance such as bonds, especially because developers often sell projects to regulated utilities. On attorney fees, Phillips confirmed that in executed JDAs the developer has always paid county attorney fees, though fee caps are currently being contested in pending negotiations. Supervisor Hau asked about health damage compensation and energy going off the local grid; Phillips said neither falls within county authority and landowner health concerns should be addressed between landowners and the developer directly. Supervisor Dickinson questioned why a developer with no legal obligation to sign a JDA would do so, with Phillips responding that a signed JDA allows the developer to check off PSC decision-matrix issue number three — compliance with orderly land use — but that the other 10 PSC decision criteria still must be addressed independently.

Motion to Forward JDA Discussion to Full County Board

Vice Chair Boots moved to forward to the full county board a recommendation to discuss whether to pursue a potential JDA, stating the full 38-member board should have input on a decision of this magnitude. Supervisor Morash seconded. Debate followed, with Supervisor Griner expressing concern that the committee had focused almost entirely on JDA options without adequately exploring other tools listed in the packet. Supervisor Morash said a moratorium was not a viable option given the existing ordinance and that all PSC-reviewed projects have been approved regardless of intervention. Supervisor Robinson agreed the sequence of decisions mattered — noting that pursuing a JDA does not guarantee Alliance will come to the table. Supervisor Dickinson noted the committee had not discussed the comprehensive plan amendment option in depth. Chair Gibbs clarified the motion: the board would discuss whether to pursue JDA negotiations, and any resulting agreement would still require future action. The motion carried, though the tally was not stated.
