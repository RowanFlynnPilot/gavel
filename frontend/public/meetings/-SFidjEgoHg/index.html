Marathon County's Joint Extension, Education & Economic Development Committee and Human Resources, Finance & Property Committee held a joint meeting to hear a proposal from New Heights Climbing and Fitness co-founder Tia Koval requesting transfer of three River Drive properties (210, 212, and 326 River Drive) for $1 in exchange for an estimated $6 million private investment to create a climbing gym, fitness center, and event venue. After the presentation, public comment, and extensive questioning about the business plan, financing, and competitive market, both committees voted to go into closed session to discuss negotiation strategies for the River Drive properties and divestment of the Thomas Street property.

Discussion

Approval of June 4th, 2026 Meeting Minutes

A motion was made and seconded by Vice Chair Cavalli to approve the minutes of the June 4th, 2026 Extension Education and Economic Development Committee meeting. The motion passed on a voice vote with no discussion.

New Heights Climbing and Fitness River Drive Proposal Presentation

Co-founder Tia Koval presented a proposal requesting the $1 transfer of 210, 212, and 326 River Drive from Marathon County in exchange for a projected $6 million private investment. The plan includes a bouldering and rope climbing gym, a 24-hour fitness center, the Ethos event venue, and co-working space, with full operations projected by Q4 2027. Koval said the project is primarily financed through an SBA loan with Incredible Bank, supplemented by private equity, potential TIF assistance from the City of Wausau, and WEDC grants. EBITDA projections range from $361,000 in Year 1 to $1.28 million in Year 5.

Committee Questions on Financing and Business Plan

Supervisors raised significant questions about the proposal's financial details. One supervisor asked why EBITDA rather than net income was used in projections and whether capital expenditure plans were complete. Supervisor Nice requested a comprehensive business plan, feasibility study with demographics, detailed financial statements, and preliminary construction plans. Chair Kennedy noted that operating expenses appeared to increase only 1.6% over four years while revenue nearly doubled, calling the figures questionable. Supervisor Krems asked whether Koval would object if a competing facility later sought similar county assistance; Koval said she did not think Wausau's population could support more than one climbing gym. Koval acknowledged that TIF financing had not been advanced due to the lack of a confirmed location, and that committed private investors exist but were not named in the business plan.

Public Comment — Kim Winterhoff

Kim Winterhoff, a local business owner and real estate investor, spoke in strong support of the proposal. She argued the facility should be viewed as a long-term economic anchor and community center rather than just a rock climbing gym, citing demand from Central Wisconsin residents who currently drive to Appleton or Green Bay to climb. She vouched personally for Tia Koval and Steve Arnold's craftsmanship and business competence, and urged the committee to consider long-term tourism revenue, workforce retention, and youth programming benefits rather than treating the property transfer as a simple asset transaction.

RFP Process and Property Divestment Discussion

County staff Chris [Holman] confirmed the RFP was issued April 23rd with a July due date, drew approximately six attendees at the first walk-through, and received only one formal proposal — from New Heights. Outreach included press releases, radio interviews, online posting, and emails to approximately 40 developers. Supervisor Naxer confirmed this was the first RFP issued for these properties. Supervisor Michael raised concerns about the county's obligation to maximize taxpayer return, while county administration explained that the RFP process was designed to evaluate broader community benefit criteria rather than highest dollar value, consistent with direction the board had previously provided. County Administrator Leonard noted that the manner in which properties were acquired can dictate how they are sold, and that closed session was appropriate to discuss negotiation strategy.

Motion to Go Into Closed Session

Board Chair Kurt Gibbs moved to go into closed session under Wisconsin Statute 19.85(1)(e) for competitive or bargaining reasons, to discuss strategies for negotiating a River Drive property contract and divestment of the Thomas Street property. Both the HR Finance & Property Committee and the Extension Education & Economic Development Committee voted separately by roll call to enter closed session. Online supervisors were informed they would be placed in a lobby until the closed session concluded.

Action items

  • New Heights Climbing and Fitness to provide a comprehensive business plan for each business entity involved
  • New Heights to provide a feasibility study with market demographics
  • New Heights to provide more detailed financial statements including net income, depreciation, taxes, and capital expenditures
  • New Heights to provide preliminary construction plans and cost estimates
  • Both committees to convene in closed session to discuss River Drive negotiation strategy and Thomas Street divestment strategy
