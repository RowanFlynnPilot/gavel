City Council Human Resources Committee Meeting

August 10, 2026

The Wausau City Council Human Resources Committee met on August 10th and approved two HR-related reclassifications: elevating the HR Specialist position to HR Generalist with a pay increase, and approving a temporary planning intern for the comprehensive plan update. The committee also approved organizational restructuring and succession planning changes for the Department of Public Works and Utilities on a 4-1 vote, over objections from Alderwoman Larson who argued the changes would not address underlying problems in the department.

Discussion

Approval of Minutes

A motion to approve the minutes of the preceding month was made by Alder Wiskowski and seconded by Alder Kilian. The motion passed on a voice vote with no opposition noted.

Reclassification of Human Resources Specialist to Human Resources Generalist

Interim HR Director Ann Keenan presented the reclassification, explaining that the incumbent (Jennifer) had long been performing generalist-level duties including workforce planning, labor relations, FMLA, compliance, and HRIS administration — well beyond a specialist's narrow scope. Several committee members, including Alder Kilian and Alder Wiskowski, raised concerns about whether the change should wait until a new HR Director was hired, worried that a new director might want to restructure the department differently. Alderwoman Mertens argued the incumbent deserved to be compensated at the level she was already performing. After debate, Keenan argued the reclassification was already part of former director Lisa's vision and that the employee was doing the work regardless of title. The committee ultimately voted to approve the reclassification, which will carry a wage increase and move to the finance committee.

Approval of Creation of Planning Intern Position

Planner Carrie [last name not stated] presented a request for a temporary part-time planning intern, approximately 15 hours per week at roughly $16/hour for about four months, estimated total cost no more than $5,000, funded from already-budgeted comprehensive plan funds. The intern would assist with public engagement data collection and aggregation for the city's comprehensive plan update. Alder Kilian questioned whether the incoming administrative assistant could handle the work, and Alder Wiskowski asked about measurable benefits to the city. Alderwoman Mertens clarified the cost would come from already-allocated comprehensive plan funds — not new budget dollars — which resolved some concerns. The committee approved the position on a voice vote.

Organizational Structure Changes and Succession Planning for Department of Public Works and Utilities

DPW Director Eric Lindman presented proposed reclassifications: building inspector reclassification, fleet manager to fleet superintendent, and traffic maintainers to traffic control specialists. He also proposed eliminating a difficult-to-fill part-time facilities maintenance position and reassigning two equipment operators from streets to report directly under the facilities manager, projecting a net savings of approximately $9,000 per year. Alderwoman Larson opposed the item, arguing it would not address what she described as serious ongoing problems in the DPW department that she said were expected to come before the committee in the future; she moved to table until the next meeting. A point of order was raised and debated. Alder Kilian argued the succession planning was not related to broader departmental issues and supported moving forward. The motion to approve passed 4-1, with Alderwoman Larson casting the dissenting vote.

HR Department Staffing Concerns

Following the DPW vote, Alder Wiskowski raised concerns about the HR department operating with only two staff members while Interim Director Keenan and specialist Jennifer manage an overwhelming workload including firefighter hiring, crossing guard recruitment, benefits expansion, and Workday system administration. Keenan acknowledged the department was 'at critical' staffing levels and noted that one FMLA event would leave them with a single employee. She said she planned to bring a request to the full City Council on Wednesday to accelerate hiring of the HR coordinator position rather than waiting for a new director. The committee encouraged Keenan not to suffer in silence and asked whether a temp hire might help bridge the gap.
