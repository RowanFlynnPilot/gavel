HR, Finance & Property Committee - New Heights Redevelopment Proposal

The committee reached consensus not to accept the New Heights proposal for property redevelopment at this time. The chair summarized that administration would communicate with New Heights regarding additional information requirements and that the property would remain open for redevelopment. New Heights could return to the agenda once additional information was provided. The committee then adjourned, with a motion moved and seconded by Hart, approved unanimously.

2027 Budget Development - Rates, Fees, and Revenue Update

County Administrator Lance Leonard reported that the Department of Revenue released preliminary net new construction numbers at 1.54%, roughly 10% below the county's budget projection of 1.65%, meaning less revenue than anticipated. Staff circulated spreadsheets to department heads with a built-in 3% fee increase assumption, asking them to flag statutory limitations, areas where 3% doesn't cover costs, and fees where business reasons argue against increases. Leonard noted that unlike private businesses, county government cannot charge more than the cost of service delivery. A supervisor asked how child care ranked in the budget prioritization exercise, and Leonard said the $50,000 child care continuation proposal ranked highest among all new discretionary projects.

Marshfield Area Chamber of Commerce Partnership Request - $25,000

Denise Sam, executive director of the Marshfield Area Chamber of Commerce and Industry, presented the chamber's work serving a 10-15 mile radius including Marathon County communities like Stratford, Spencer, and Rozellville, and requested $25,000 annually matching what Wood County provides. Supervisor Krums questioned how many other chambers serve Marathon County and whether the county contributes to any of them; Administrator Leonard noted the county is a member of Greater Wausau Chamber and that the airport belongs to the Mosinee chamber, but the county does not provide economic development funds to chambers. A supervisor raised concerns about redundancy with MCDEVCO and the county's limited discretionary spending, stating objection to the request. MCDEVCO Executive Director Kim Weber suggested the better path was a referral relationship rather than separate funding. Because the item was listed as an educational presentation, no action could be taken.

MCDEVCO Monthly Report

MCDEVCO Executive Director Kim Weber reported that all $1 million of the Marathon County Revolving Loan Fund has been deployed into the community, with $962,000 dispersed last year. She highlighted loan recipients including Redwood Street Roasters in Edgar ($250,000), Sugar 'n Spice Cheesecakes, Peking Restaurant, and The Story Seller. The Pelzer Revolving Loan Fund for low-income rural septic system replacement has assisted over 17 systems with $256,000 disbursed and $250,000 remaining. The Wausau Business Incubator is 92% full across office, manufacturing, and kitchen spaces. Weber noted upcoming programming including an accounting workshop in partnership with NTC and UWSP and a personal financial statement lunch and learn with the Wausau River District.

Child Caring Scholarship Program Update and $50,000 Continuation Proposal

Kelly from Child Caring reported that of the 30 scholarships funded by the county's $200,000 American Rescue Plan Act allocation (contracted October 2024, starting January 2025), 22 have been awarded and 20 individuals have completed training and been job-placed in child care settings, supporting approximately 350 children. Eight scholarships remain and three people are already enrolled in a new class. A proposed $50,000 continuation would fund 10 additional teachers at $4,000 each ($40,000 in scholarships, $10,000 to Child Caring for administration), and Child Caring has separately submitted a $50,000 grant request to the Women's Fund through the Community Foundation to potentially match county funds and support 20 total new teachers. Supervisor Lamers and Chair Gibbs expressed strong support. The loss of Child Care Counts subsidy funding at end of June—potentially millions of dollars in Marathon County—has already caused six programs and 60 slots to close in the last three months, with 15-20% cost increases to families anticipated.

UW-Extension Monthly Report and Upcoming Local Officials Workshop

Extension staff reported participation in Farm Technology Days in Stratford in early July and the ongoing Marathon County Fair. Horticulture Educator Janelle Ware described an upcoming multi-disciplinary local officials workshop focused on nature-based solutions to reduce weather-related impacts and costs, designed as a networking event to connect government agencies across departments and disciplines so that low-cost mitigation opportunities can be acted on more effectively.
