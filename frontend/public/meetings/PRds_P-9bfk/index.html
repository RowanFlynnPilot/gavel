Approval of July 14, 2026 Meeting Minutes

The chair called for a motion to approve the minutes. A motion was made by Gellhar and seconded by Hadley. The minutes were approved by voice vote with no corrections noted.

Fluoride Supply Shortage

Staff identified as Scott reported that the utility received a fluoride shipment last month but was advised the next shipment may not arrive until late next month, prompting a reduction of fluoride dosing to approximately half the normal level, consistent with actions taken by larger communities facing the same shortage. Staff noted the DNR has approved the approach and that staff is attempting to reach the county health department director for guidance on whether to notify dental offices, schools, and other entities of the reduction. One commissioner noted the CDC has been working on fluoride elimination since April, to which the chair responded the commission had previously heard public debate on fluoride.

GAC Carbon Vessel Change-Out and PFAS Performance

Scott reported that the first GAC carbon change-out on August 5–6 went well, with Carbon Activated Corporation praised for professionalism, and that the next change-out is scheduled for September 3 using regenerated carbon from the first vessel. Staff noted that carbon saturation is occurring faster than anticipated — the vessels are not lasting the expected two years on regenerated carbon — and that change-outs will need to be expedited. Staff explained they are monitoring influent PFAS levels, which have remained stable, and are working to find the operational 'sweet spot' to maximize carbon longevity while staying under the 4 parts per trillion PFAS limit required by 2029. A commissioner asked whether influent PFAS concentrations are increasing; staff confirmed they are not.

CDM Smith Corrosion Control Treatment Study Presentation

CDM Smith representatives Amu Itasi and Javier Lockson presented findings from a roughly year-and-a-half pipe loop study required by the Wisconsin DNR due to Wausau's treatment process change. Key findings included: the new treatment plant did not significantly change lead mineral phases on pipe scale; fifth-liter sampling (required under the new Lead and Copper Rule Improvements effective 2028) showed a 90th percentile lead concentration of 19.2 micrograms per liter, exceeding both current and upcoming action levels, versus 14.2 for first-liter sampling which would have kept the system in compliance; and orthophosphate at 1 mg/L produced the best results, reducing lead concentrations and variability without the particulate lead release seen at 3 mg/L. The DNR accepted the study and directed Wausau to begin dosing orthophosphate at 1 mg/L with the system operational by July 2028, though CDM Smith recommended having it online by spring 2026 to allow 8–12 months for the treatment to take effect before new sampling requirements begin. Commissioners asked about health effects of orthophosphate (deemed safe and widely used since the 1990s), impacts on wastewater phosphorus loads (existing plant already designed to handle phosphorus removal), biological growth risks in the distribution system, and whether the existing sodium silicate corrosion control would continue alongside orthophosphate (confirmed yes, for now).

PFAS Process Waste Tank and Chemical Room Project Proposal

Director Eric explained that $1.667 million in federal grant funds originally earmarked for carbon change-outs can no longer be used for that purpose and must be applied to a capital project. The proposal calls for an underground process waste tank — needed because the existing 100,000-gallon tank causes plant operational lockouts during frequent carbon change-out backwashing — plus a chemical room addition for orthophosphate dosing and a storage room convertible to a future chemical room. The total project is estimated at approximately $2.5 million, leaving a projected shortfall of $700,000–$800,000 after the grant. Susan from Donahue Engineering confirmed the estimate. Staff noted a pending EPA verification on the competitive procurement process used to select Donahue. Commissioners were told an updated capital plan would be presented by Aylers on September 1st. Motion to approve was made by Hadley and seconded by Van Craig; the proposal was approved by voice vote.
